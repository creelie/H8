# Computations

This document accompanies the paper *Density of Algebraic Loci of Weil Classes on Abelian Varieties* by Deep Bhattacharjee, Priyabrata Mandal and Ushashi Bhattacharya. The paper cites the programs of this archive as [BMB26]. Below, each computation is listed under the item number that the programs, `code/verify_all.py` and the README use, with the results of the paper that it checks. Theorem, section and equation numbers refer to the compiled paper `tex/main.pdf`.

Every numerical assertion in this paper is checked in exact arithmetic:
rational, integer or exterior-algebra computations, and exact linear algebra
over number fields and over function fields in one variable, with one
exception: item (LXXII) evaluates integrals of theta functions by quadrature
in double precision and reads a rank off from the ratio of the smallest to
the largest singular value, which it compares with the change of the entries
under a finer grid. A rank computed modulo a prime is used only
as a lower bound for the rank over $\mathbb{Q}$, since reduction can only lower a
rank, and the bound is matched by an exact computation in the other
direction. Items (XXIX), (XXXI) and (XL) are $\operatorname{Ext}$
computations over a polynomial ring. The programs that carry out the
computations are in the directory `code/` of this archive, and the driver
`verify_all.py` runs them item by item. For each item we describe what is
computed, how it is computed, and which statements of the paper it supports.

## The items

### (I) The Fourier–Mukai transform on powers of the polarisation

Checks: Remark 6.6.

The exterior algebra $\bigwedge^{*}(V\oplus V^{\vee})$ of Notation 6.2 is constructed explicitly for $g\le4$, with a basis indexed by subsets of the generators and structure constants given by the shuffle sign. The class $\ell$ of (18), the forms $\theta$ and $\theta'$ of (16) and (17), and the exponential $e^{\ell}$ are formed, and $\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}(\theta'^{\,k})$ is computed by extracting the coefficient of the top form in the dual variables. The result is an exact rational multiple of $\theta^{\,g-k}$ with no other component, and the multiple agrees with (19) in every case.

### (II) The sign

Checks: Remark 6.6, Lemma A.1.

The proof of Theorem 6.5 uses that the sign $\sigma(g,k)$ is the same for all index sets $T$. This is checked as a permutation computation for every $g\le12$ and every $0\le k\le g$, together with the closed form (22); all values agree.

### (III) The character separation

The grading (15) is formed for $n\le4$ and the positions of the Weil line and of $\eta^{n}$ are computed, confirming Lemma 5.2, Lemma 5.3 and the disjointness of Theorem 5.6.

### (IV) The numerical comparison

The values of $2n^{2}+2n$, $2^{2n}$ and $3^{2n}$ are tabulated for $2\le n\le8$, confirming Lemma 8.2. As counterexamples to the inference discussed in Remark 8.5, the item also records the polarisation types $(1,3)$ and $(1,5)$ on an abelian surface, with Euler characteristics $3$ and $5$.

### (V) The semiregularity target

Checks: Remark 17.7.

The identity $\sum_{q}\binom{2n}{q}\binom{2n}{q+2}=\binom{4n}{2n-2}$ of Proposition 9.2 is verified for $2\le n\le7$, with the growth rates of Corollary 9.3.

### (VI) The coordinate model

Checks: Section 4, Remark 4.10, Proposition 17.10, Proposition 17.11, Remark C.8.

The model of Setup 4.1 is built over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$ for the pairs $(n,d)$ of a table covering $n\le3$ and $d\in\{1,2,3,5,7\}$, and eight statements are checked for each: that the $u^{s}_{j}$ of (7) are eigenvectors of $S$; that $\omega_{+}+\omega_{-}$ and $(\omega_{+}-\omega_{-})/\delta$ are rational and agree with the closed form (11); that $\iota(\tau)^{*}$ multiplies $\omega_{\pm}$ by $\tau^{2n}$ and $\overline{\tau}^{\,2n}$ at five values of $\tau$; that $\omega_{\pm}$ is of Hodge type $(n,n)$ for the balanced structure and of type $(p,2n-p)$ for every other one, which is Proposition C.7; that $\eta$ is rational, of type $(1,1)$ and a norm eigenclass; that the two Riemann relations hold with the sign vector of Setup 4.1; that $\eta^{2n}\ne0$; and that the supports of $\omega_{1},\omega_{2}$ and $\eta^{n}$ are disjoint, which is Proposition 4.11.

### (VII) The Hodge class count

Checks: Section 4, Theorem 4.14.

For $n\le4$ and every $(r,s)$, the dimension of the space of $\mathfrak{sl}_{2n}$-invariants in $\bigwedge^{r}V_{+}\otimes\bigwedge^{s}V_{+}^{\vee}$ is computed in exact rational arithmetic, as the kernel of the raising operators on the weight-zero subspace. This gives $\dim\mathrm{Hdg}^{n}=3$ and $\dim\mathrm{Hdg}^{k}=1$ for $k\ne n$, which is Theorem 4.14, and the codimension count of Proposition B.8.

### (VIII) The Clifford algebra

Checks: Section 11.

The algebra $C(T,q)$ is built for forms of signature $(3,b-3)$ with $b\le8$. The computation checks the Clifford relations and $v^{2}=q(v)$ for a general $v$; that $J^{2}=-1$ and that left multiplication by $J$ squares to $-\operatorname{id}$ on $C^{+}$, which is Lemma 11.4; and that $C^{-}C^{+}C^{-}\subseteq C^{+}$, which is why the map $\Psi$ of Proposition 11.7 takes values in $\operatorname{End}(C^{+})$. It also tabulates the Hodge numbers of Proposition 11.10, together with the solution $n=1$ of Proposition 11.11.

### (IX) The family and the secant plane

The computations verify Theorem 14.2 symbolically for $n\le5$ and several $d$, and derive the closed forms of Theorem 14.5 and Theorem 14.7.

### (X) The split member

Checks: Example 15.3.

The computation builds $A=X\times\widehat{X}$ with the action (28) in coordinates and makes three checks, the first two independent of each other. First, exact rational Gaussian elimination expresses each of $\omega_{1},\omega_{2}$ as a rational combination of the degree $2n$ monomials in $\beta,\widehat{\beta},\ell$, and the solution is recorded; this succeeds for $n\le3$ and $d\in\{1,2,3,7\}$. Second, Theorem 15.2 is verified directly: $\gamma\mp\sqrt{-d}\,\ell$ is an eigenclass for $\tau^{2}$, respectively $\overline{\tau}^{\,2}$, and (33) holds exactly, for $n\le5$ and several $d$. Third, the formulas of Example 15.3 are checked as stated, at $n=2$ for $d\le3$ and at $n=3$ for $d\in\{1,2,3,7\}$.

### (XI) The reach of the base case

Checks: Theorem 14.2, Proposition 15.19.

The computation checks the four assertions of Proposition 15.19. It verifies $M^{2}=-d$ and $M^{\top}Q+QM=0$ entry by entry for the block form $Q=\beta\oplus(\widehat\beta/d)$ on $H_{1}(A,\mathbb{Q})$, for $n\le4$, and checks, for six pairs $(n,d)$, that the action induced on $H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Q})$ multiplies $d\beta+\widehat\beta$ by $d$, multiplies $\gamma$ and $\ell$ by $-d$, and has $\beta+d\widehat\beta$ as an eigenclass only when $d=1$. It computes $\det H=(-d)^{n}(d_{1}\cdots d_{n})^{2}$ for ten choices of $(n,d,\beta)$, including non-principal $\beta$, and certifies in each case that $(-1)^{n}\det H$ is a norm from $K$, together with the two facts the proof uses, that $\det B$ is a square and that $d$ is a norm. It checks the codimension formula $n^{2}-n(n+1)/2=n(n-1)/2$ for $n\le10$. Finally it computes both the dimension of the space of $\mathrm{Sp}$-invariants and the rank of the set of monomials $\beta^{i}\widehat{\beta}^{j}\ell^{k}$ in degree $2n$, finding rank three in degree two for $n\le4$ and agreement in the middle degree for $n\le3$.

### (XII) Quaternionic multiplication

Checks: Remark 16.25.

The computation builds the quaternion algebra $B=(-d,b)$ and the module $B^{n}$ in coordinates and verifies Theorem 15.25 for nine choices of $(n,d,b,a)$ with $n\le3$: the relations $i^{2}=-d$, $j^{2}=b$, $ij=-ji$; that $E$ of (37) is alternating and nondegenerate with $i$ anti-self-adjoint and $j$ self-adjoint; that the hermitian form on the $K$-basis is $\mathrm{diag}(2a_{k}d,-2a_{k}bd)$ with signature $(n,n)$; the determinant formula and hence the class $b^{n}$; and that $g(x,y)=E(jx,y)$ is alternating, $K$-bilinear and nondegenerate, which is the hypothesis of Theorem 15.16. It then checks Theorem 15.27 numerically: for each of several fields, every nontrivial class in the tested range is realised in every dimension $n\le8$ with signature $(n,n)$, by a single factor when $n$ is odd and by the product of Lemma 15.26 when $n$ is even. Finally it records the two dimensions behind Remark 16.25: the quaternionic locus has dimension $n(n+1)/2$ inside a period domain of dimension $n^{2}$, so its codimension is $n(n-1)/2$, which is checked for $n\le10$ and is $1$ exactly at $n=2$.

### (XIII) The semiregularity map of a sum of line bundles

Checks: Section 9.2, Remark 18.8, Corollary 18.13, Proposition 18.14, (47), (50).

The computation builds the split member with $X=E_{i}^{n}$, so that the Hodge decomposition is defined over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ and every step is exact, and verifies the following. The six degree four monomials in $\beta,\widehat\beta,\ell$ are independent for $n\le4$, which is Lemma 18.3. For six pairs $(n,d)$, $\theta^{+}\theta^{-}$ is never a multiple of $\eta^{2}$, which is Lemma 18.4. The coefficient of $\theta^{+}\theta^{-}$ in $\operatorname{ch}_{2}$ is $\sum_{i}m_{i}\mathrm{N}(u_{i})$, the identity behind Theorem 18.5, and for five pairs $(n,d)$ no multiset with positive multiplicities satisfies the resulting equations with some $u_{i}$ nonzero. The map $\Phi$ of Lemma 18.1(iii) is injective at $n=2$ for $s\le3$ and has rank $22$ rather than $24$ at $s=4$. An exhaustive search over the classes $a\beta+b\widehat\beta+c\ell$ with $|a|,|b|,|c|\le3$ and $d\in\{1,2\}$ finds no signed tuple of at most three line bundles with a flat Chern character and a nonzero Weil component, and every signed tuple of at most four in the same box with these two properties has exactly four terms and a map $\Phi$ with nonzero kernel, of dimension $2$, $3$, $4$ or $6$ at $d=1$ and $3$ or $6$ at $d=2$. At $n=3$ the computation finds the Prouhet–Tarry–Escott pair of Lemma 18.11 on the norm circle $\mathrm{N}=4$ of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ by exhaustive search, and verifies that the six line bundles of Proposition 18.14 have $\operatorname{ch}_{k}=0$ for $k\ne3$ and $\operatorname{ch}_{3}=12\,\omega_{1}$ and that every difference of the six classes is nondegenerate of index three. A second computation repeats the rank computations modulo a prime congruent to $1$ modulo $4$, which reaches $n=3$: it reproduces the $n=2$ ranks exactly, finds $\Phi$ injective at $n=3$ for a general choice of up to ten classes, and finds rank $75$ of $90$ for the explicit object.

### (XIV) The annihilator and the rank-zero obstruction

Checks: Section 9.2, Lemma 9.6, Corollary 9.8, Remark 9.9.

Modulo the same prime, the annihilator of each rational generator of the Weil line in $H^{0,2}$ has dimension $n^{2}$ at $n=2,3,4$, which is Lemma 9.6. The computation splits the semiregularity map of the explicit object of Section 14 by parity and finds an odd kernel of dimension $9=n^{2}$ equal to the annihilator, an even kernel of dimension $6$, and a total of $15$, against $0$ for a generic triple, which is Corollary 9.8. It then computes the semiregularity rank for every configuration $s_{i}\gamma+c_{i}\ell$ with $\sum s_{i}=\sum c_{i}=0$ in a box of side $7$ whose virtual Chern character lies on the Weil line: there are eighteen such configurations, and the rank is $75$ of $90$ for all of them, which is Remark 9.9.

### (XV) The Weil class under products

Checks: (35).

The computation verifies, in exact arithmetic over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$, the two identities (35) for every pair $n_{1},n_{2}\le3$; their $K$-valued form $\omega(A_{1})\omega(A_{2})=2\,\omega(A_{1}\times A_{2})$ over the same range; the $k$-factor identity with constant $2^{k-1}$ for six partitions with $k=3$ and $k=4$; that the Weil class lies in $H^{2n}$, so that at $n=1$ it is a divisor class, which is Lemma 15.9; and the dimension comparison of Remark 15.11, that the product locus has dimension $n$ inside a family of dimension $n^{2}$ and is smaller than the quaternionic locus for every $n\ge2$.

### (XVI) The annihilator as the tangent space

The computation builds a polarised Hodge structure of $(K,-1,n)$-Weil type directly over the Gaussian rationals, with no reduction modulo a prime, and checks five statements at $n=2,3,4$. First, the construction is what it claims to be: the polarisation has exactly two nonzero blocks on $V_{+}\times V_{-}$, each a perfect pairing of $n$-dimensional spaces, and the two rational generators of the Weil line are real of type $(n,n)$. Second, the annihilator of each generator in $H^{0,2}$ has dimension exactly $n^{2}$, which is Lemma 9.6 again, in a model built independently of the one used in item (XIV). Third, the polarisation-preserving first-order deformations have dimension $n(2n+1)=\dim\mathcal{A}_{2n}$, and those commuting with $K$ have dimension $n^{2}=\dim\mathcal{D}_{n,n}$. Fourth, a polarised deformation contracts the whole Weil line to zero exactly on that $n^{2}$-dimensional subspace, and one generator already cuts it out, which is Proposition 9.14. Fifth, the map of Theorem 9.17 is injective, independent of the basis of $V_{+}^{1,0}$ used to write it, and has image exactly the annihilator. The five statements are checked again for eight distinct choices of the $n$-plane $V_{+}^{1,0}$ at $n=2$ and at $n=3$, and hold in all sixteen.

### (XVII) The quaternionic locus as a Lagrangian Grassmannian

Checks: Proposition 15.32.

The computation verifies, over $\mathbb{Q}$ in exact arithmetic and for $2\le n\le5$, the three statements of Proposition 15.32. First, the form $\omega_{\psi}(x,y)=E(x,\psi y)$ attached to a Rosati-symmetric $\psi$ is alternating on $V_{+}$, which is the identity $E(\psi x,y)=E(x,\psi y)$ read twice. Second, the graph of a symmetric form is isotropic and the graph of a form that is not symmetric is not, so the chart of $\mathrm{LG}(n,2n)$ by symmetric forms describes the locus exactly and is not merely contained in it. Third, the chart has dimension $n(n+1)/2$, computed as the rank of the symmetric basis rather than quoted, so that the codimension in $\mathcal{D}_{n,n}$ is $n(n-1)/2$; at $n=4$ these are $10$ and $6$. The computation also checks that a random $n$-plane, completed to a basis of $V_{+}$, is Lagrangian for the form that is standard in that basis, which is the construction behind Remark 15.33: over the reals the loci sweep out the whole family, and only the rationality of $\psi$ prevents them from covering it.

### (XVIII) The size forced on an object carrying the class

Checks: Proposition 9.11.

In the exterior algebra over $\mathbb{Q}$, for $1\le n\le4$, the computation verifies that $\alpha_{\pm}^{2}=0$, hence $\omega_{1}^{2}=2\alpha_{+}\alpha_{-}$, that $\int\omega_{1}^{2}\ne0$, and that $\chi(E,E)=(-1)^{n}N^{2}\int\omega^{2}$ for every rank $r$ and every multiplicity $N$ in the ranges tested, the rank cancelling out of the identity. This is Proposition 9.11, and with it the lower bound $\sum_{i}\dim\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(E,E)\ge N^{2}|\int\omega^{2}|$. The constant is not dimension-free, since it depends on the normalisation of $\omega$ against $\eta$, so no growth rate in $n$ should be read off it; the bound needs only that the Weil class is not isotropic.

### (XIX) Infinitesimal rigidity of divisor classes

Checks: (25).

The computation builds the four blocks of $H^{1,1}$ and the tangent space of $\mathcal{D}_{n,n}$ over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ and verifies, for $n=2,3,4$, the three parts of Proposition 9.15. First, the real span of the classes killed by contraction has dimension exactly one and is the polarisation; this is checked against the full real space of $(1,1)$ classes, of dimension $4n^{2}$, which is $16$ at $n=2$ and $64$ at $n=4$. Second, the kernel attached to a norm-character class with block $a$ has dimension $n(n-\operatorname{rank}a)$ for every rank; at $n=4$ the four values are $12$, $8$, $4$, $0$. Third, a class drawn at random has kernel zero, so it rigidifies the family to a point. The dimension $n^{2}$ of the tangent space is recomputed in the same computation rather than assumed.

### (XX) The invariants of the required object

Checks: Proposition 15.30.

For $1\le n\le8$, every squarefree $d\le11$ and every rank and twist in a range, the Chern classes of the Chern character $au+bv$ are computed by Newton's identities, and every one of them is integral. The computation also confirms the closed form $\Delta=a^{2}d+b^{2}$ of Theorem 14.7 and its positivity. This is Proposition 15.30: nothing numerical forbids the object the construction needs, in any dimension tested.

### (XXI) Existence of a sheaf with the required Chern character

Checks: Remark 14.14.

The computation behind Theorem 14.13 is carried out exactly over $\mathbb{Q}$ for $1\le n\le10$. For every squarefree $d\le11$ and every rank and twist in a range, it solves the Vandermonde system that expresses the rational point $au_{t}+bv_{t}$ of the secant plane as a rational combination of the Chern characters $e^{jt}=\operatorname{ch}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(jt))$, clears denominators to obtain the least positive multiple $M$ and the integer witness (31), and then verifies that witness independently of the solution, by recomputing every moment $\sum_{j}n_{j}j^{k}$ and comparing it with $Mc_{k}$. It checks that the rank $\sum_{j}n_{j}$ of the witness equals $Ma$, which is the positive rank hypothesis of Lemma 14.12, and it records the least $M$ in each case: $6$ at $n=4$ with $d=3$ and $a=b=1$, at most $2016$ over the whole range at $n=8$, and never $1$ once $n\ge7$, which is Remark 14.14. Finally it expands $\theta^{k}/k!$ in the exterior algebra of a symplectic lattice and finds every coefficient integral; this is why the coordinates $c_{k}$ are integers, and hence why the problem is a lattice problem.

### (XXII) Semiregular split objects

Checks: (51).

The computation settles Theorem 18.20 in exact integer arithmetic. It enumerates the norms represented by $\mathcal{O}_{K}$ below a bound, forms for every set of $n+1$ of them the forced level sums (51) and the least integral scale, and tests them against the supply $r_{K}(N_{j})$ of elements of that norm; at $n+2$ norms the solution space is two-dimensional, and its integer points in the same box are swept coordinate by coordinate. For $4\le n\le8$ and $d\le19$ nothing survives, with norms up to $110$ at $n=4$ and up to $30$ at $n=8$. At $n=3$ and norms up to $200$ exactly eleven sets survive, all at $d=1$ or $d=2$, of the three shapes recorded in Theorem 18.20(iii). The computation then runs the whole system (44) on those sets: for each it enumerates every assignment of signs to the elements of $\mathcal{O}_{K}$ at those norms with the prescribed level sums, splitting the four levels into two halves, one tabulated and the other enumerated against the table, so that the enumeration is complete; it finds no solution for eight of the sets. A second computation settles the other three, $d=1$ at $(13,52,117,130)$, $(17,68,153,170)$ and $(25,50,100,125)$, whose largest level has sixteen elements: it matches three levels against the fourth through a linear hash of the ten off-diagonal coordinates, re-checks every match exactly, and finds no solution. As a control it also reruns two of the eight. The largest search covers $4.29\times10^{14}$ sign patterns.

### (XXIII) The quaternionic Weil cycle

Checks: Proposition 16.19.

The computation checks Proposition 16.19 on the lattice $\mathcal{O}_{b}^{2}$. It confirms the ratios $-b/3$ and $4b^{2}/3$ and the value $1/12$ for $d\in\{1,3\}$, two weight vectors and fifteen values of $b$ up to $101$; the decomposition $E_{b}=E_{u}+bE_{v}$, $g_{b}=bg_{1}$, $\lambda_{b}=b\lambda_{1}$ with $g_{1}$ and $\lambda_{1}$ integral; the equalities $Z_{b}=b^{2}Z_{1}$ and $Z'_{b}=b^{2}Z'_{1}$ in $\wedge^{4}\Lambda^{*}$; the growth $\int\eta_{b}^{4}=b^{2}\int\eta_{1}^{4}$; and the effect of replacing $j$ by $\alpha j$. It then computes the saturation of the Weil plane in $\wedge^{4}\Lambda^{*}$ and the sublattice spanned by products of two integral divisor classes, for $d\in\{1,2,3,7\}$, three weight vectors and seven values of $b$, and finds Gram matrix $\mathrm{diag}(8d,8d^{2})$ and index $2(a_{1}a_{2})^{2}$ throughout.

### (XXIV) The divisor route on a fixed lattice

Checks: Proposition 16.24.

The computation checks Proposition 16.24 on $\Lambda=\mathcal{O}^{2}$ with $j^{2}=1$, for $d\in\{1,3\}$. ## The Lean certificate

The theorems of this paper are statements of algebraic geometry and are not formalised. What is formalised is the finite arithmetic on which many of them turn, which the kernel can settle by computation; the rest of their computational content is checked in exact arithmetic by the computations of the items above. The certificate is a single Lean 4 file that accompanies the paper. It compiles against a bare Lean 4 toolchain, contains no `sorry`, and depends on no library, Mathlib included. ### Failure of the obvious witness

 Two theorems of the file show that statements a careful reader would accept are false. Both bear on Section 5.
 The first concerns the witness $\tau=1+\sqrt{-d}$ for Lemma 5.4. It is the obvious choice, and it does not work for every field: `bad_witness` shows that at $d=1$ the ratio $\tau/\overline{\tau}$ is $\sqrt{-1}$ and at $d=3$ it is a primitive cube root of unity, so for those fields the two characters agree at this $\tau$ for suitable $n$. The lemma is true, but not by that route; this is why its proof in Section 5 uses only the finiteness of $\mu(K)$ and avoids witnesses altogether.
 The second concerns the exceptional set of that witness at $d=3$. It is $3\mid n$, and not $6\mid n$ as the order of $\mu(K)$ might suggest; `bad_witness_exceptions` records the exact condition. Neither statement affects Theorem 5.6. Both are slips of the kind that a careful reading can miss and a kernel check catches, and the file keeps them for that reason.


### Limits of the formalisation

 Everything else is used as mathematics and is not formalised here: the cohomology of an abelian variety as an exterior algebra, the Hodge decomposition, the Fourier–Mukai transform as a functor, Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch, the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem, hard Lefschetz, the invariant theory of $\operatorname{SL}_{2n}$, the Clifford algebra as a functor, and the Lefschetz standard conjecture. Formalising them would require a library of complex algebraic geometry that does not at present exist.
