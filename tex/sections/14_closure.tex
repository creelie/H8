\section{Closure}\label{sec:final}

We close by stating, in a single theorem, what the paper proves and what it
leaves open. Its target, \Cref{thm:main}, is clause (vi), and the other
clauses measure the distance from it to the conjecture. Every clause of
\Cref{thm:final} is proved in the body. None of them depends on a hypothesis that is not itself proved here or quoted from the
literature with a reference, and the computation of minimal sets in the last
clause is checked by machine.

The results we quote are theorems and are used as such: the semiregularity
theorem of Buchweitz and Flenner \cite{BF03}, in the form for perfect
complexes on a deforming variety with every obstruction annihilated that
Pridham proves \cite{Pri24}; the theorem of Cattani, Deligne and Kaplan on the
locus of Hodge classes \cite{CDK95}; the description of the Hodge rings of
abelian varieties of dimension at most five by Moonen and Zarhin
\cite{MZ99}; Markman's theorems on abelian varieties of Weil type
\cite{Mar25a,Mar25b}; for the refutation in clause (iv), the theorem of Bando
and Siu on stable reflexive sheaves \cite{BS94}, Kuranishi's theorem in the
form of Goldman and Millson \cite{Kur65,GM88}, Grivaux's Chern classes of
coherent sheaves \cite{Gri10} and Voisin's theorem on Weil tori
\cite{Voi02b}; and, in the last clause, the theorems of Deligne, Andr\'e and
Milne recorded in \Cref{ssec:bullseye} \cite{Del71,And96b,Mil20} and Li's
theorem on the powers of abelian fourfolds over finite fields \cite{Li26}.
\emph{Unconditionally} in \Cref{thm:final} means that no open statement is
assumed anywhere in it; in particular, none of the statements named in its
last clause is assumed. Taken together, the clauses fix the boundary of the
subject as this paper leaves it, and that is what we mean by closure: not that
the conjecture is settled, which it is not, but that the distance between it
and what is proved here is measured rather than estimated.

\begin{theorem}[Closure]\label{thm:final}
Let $K=\QQ(\sqrt{-d})$ be imaginary quadratic with $d>0$ squarefree, let
$n\ge2$, and let $\delta\in\QQ^{\times}_{>0}/\Nm_{K/\QQ}(\Kx)$. The following
hold unconditionally.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.8em,itemsep=4pt]
\item \emph{One class.} For an abelian $2n$-fold $A$ of $(K,-1,n)$-Weil type
  with $\Hg(A)=\SU(V,H)$ one has $\dim_{\QQ}\Hdg^{k}(A)=1$ for $k\ne n$ and
  $3$ for $k=n$, and the Hodge conjecture for $A$ in every degree is the
  algebraicity of the single class $\omega_{1}$ of \eqref{eq:omegagens},
  written out with $2^{2n-1}$ integer coefficients
  (\Cref{thm:hdgcount}, \Cref{cor:whatisleft}).
\item \emph{One class, one connected domain, and no loss.} For the family of
  all members of the triple $(K,n,\delta)$ the conjecture is equivalent to the
  algebraicity of $\omega_{s}$ at every point $s$ of the period domain
  $\cD_{n,n}$, which is connected of dimension $n^{2}$. The reduction is thus
  necessary as well as sufficient (\Cref{thm:residual},
  \Cref{cor:exactform}).
\item \emph{A base point in every family.} Every triple $(K,n,\delta)$
  contains a member whose Weil classes are algebraic, with an explicit
  representative \eqref{eq:divrep} as a polynomial with rational coefficients
  in two divisor classes; the member is one with quaternionic multiplication
  extending $K$ (\Cref{thm:quatweil}, \Cref{thm:everyfamily}). So the
  existence of a base point is never the obstacle, for any field, any
  dimension or any discriminant.
\item \emph{One finite sufficient condition, never met in its pure form, and
  one criterion that is a restatement.} Suppose that at a base point $s_{0}$
  as in (iii) the semiregularity map of a perfect complex whose Chern
  character is a nonzero multiple of a rational Weil class plus a polynomial
  in the polarisation is injective. Then $\Sigma=\cD_{n,n}$, where $\Sigma$
  is the set of points $s$ at which $\omega_{s}$ is algebraic, and the Hodge
  conjecture holds in every degree for every member of the triple with full
  Hodge group (\Cref{thm:semiregclosure}, \Cref{rem:p2prime}). When the Chern
  character is exactly a multiple of the Weil class, no complex at any member
  of any family has injective semiregularity map (\Cref{thm:p2false}): it
  would deform to very general non-algebraic Weil tori, on which every
  coherent sheaf has vanishing Chern character in degrees $1$ to $2n-1$
  (\Cref{prop:weiltorussheaves}). Hence the numerical form of that
  criterion, $\dim\Ext^{2}(E,E)=2n(2n-1)$, which would suffice
  (\Cref{thm:p2numerical}) and is the least value possible
  (\Cref{cor:hhfactor}(i)), is attained by no complex, and the shape that it
  would force (\Cref{thm:p2endo}) is realised by no object. In the corrected
  form \textup{(P2$'$)}, with powers of the polarisation added, the number
  is $(4+\rho)n^{2}-2n$ for $n\ge3$, with $\rho\le3$ the rank of a Hankel
  matrix of the coefficients;\footnote{Write the Chern character as
  $N\omega_{1}+\sum_{k}c_{k}\theta^{k}$. Then $\rho$ is the rank of the
  $(2n-1)\times3$ Hankel matrix $[\mu_{i+j}]$, $0\le j\le2n-2$, $0\le i\le2$,
  of the moments $\mu_{m}=m!\,c_{m}$ (\Cref{setup:p2prime}), so that
  $\rho\le2$ exactly when the moments satisfy a linear recurrence of order at
  most two.} an object attaining it satisfies the criterion, and the shapes
  $A\,e^{t\theta}+B\,\theta^{2n}$ with $t\theta$ the class of a line bundle,
  which have Hankel rank at most two, are excluded as the pure form was
  (\Cref{thm:p2primenumber}). For every other shape and $n\ge3$ the Hodge
  locus of the character is the polarised family, so the obstruction to the
  pure form does not extend (\Cref{prop:p2primelocus}). At $n=2$ the same
  holds except at one ratio of the coefficients, where the locus is
  five-dimensional and reaches non-algebraic tori but the argument of
  \Cref{thm:p2false} breaks down at its last step
  (\Cref{rem:p2primeexceptional}). The pure form is excluded for every CM
  field (\Cref{thm:cmpurefalse}); at a quartic field and $n=2$ the least
  corrected number is $80$, taken only by the pure shape, so a complex
  meeting the criterion has $\dim\Ext^{2}(E,E)\ge88$
  (\Cref{thm:quarticrank}, \Cref{cor:quarticleast}); the Weil tori exclude
  the $\theta^{4}$ shape there, and for every other character of rank $88$
  the Hodge locus is the polarised family, so no deformation argument of this
  kind reaches it (\Cref{prop:quarticweiltori}, \Cref{prop:quarticlocus},
  \Cref{cor:quarticlocus}). The other criterion, that $\omega$ be
  represented along the orbit by cycles with bounded Hilbert data, implies
  the same conclusion (\Cref{thm:degreebound}) and is implied by it
  (\Cref{thm:p1equivalent}); it is therefore a reformulation of the
  conjecture for the family, not a route to it. Read on the transported
  cycle, the only representative that can be named, it fails whenever a
  component of the base cycle has finite stabiliser (\Cref{thm:p1forces}).
\item \emph{The routes that are closed, and in what sense.} Each of the
  following routes to the Weil class on a general member is closed, in the
  sense stated by the theorem cited and delimited in \Cref{sec:scope}: a
  construction is obstructed if it is
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},nosep,leftmargin=2.2em]
  \item one whose input has Chern character in the subring generated by
    divisor classes, transported by the Poincar\'e kernel
    (\Cref{thm:divisor});
  \item one whose output is a $\Kx$-eigenclass for the norm character
    (\Cref{thm:character});
  \item one carried by an object whose Chern character lies on the Weil line,
    whether through semiregularity or through the weakened criterion, in
    every dimension and with a kernel of dimension at least $n^{2}$ in either
    case (\Cref{thm:rankzero}, \Cref{cor:kernelfamily}, \Cref{thm:weakfails});
  \item one carried by an object with a line bundle summand off the
    polarisation line, hence by any sum of line bundles
    (\Cref{thm:linebundle}, \Cref{cor:noline});
  \item one carried by an object whose Chern character is effective at a base
    point (\Cref{thm:positivity});
  \item induction through hyperplane sections, in either direction
    (\Cref{thm:hyperplane}, \Cref{thm:returntrip});
  \item Kuga--Satake from a weight two sub-structure of $H^{2}(A,\QQ)$, which
    reaches the Weil line only at $n=1$ (\Cref{prop:normpart},
    \Cref{cor:ksboundary});
  \item a divisorial argument at a general member of a family of nontrivial
    discriminant (\Cref{prop:divfails});
  \item a secant object whose support fails to be Cohen--Macaulay at a point
    where two of its components meet and nowhere else, or at an embedded
    point, which excludes the candidate of \cite[Section 12]{Mar25b}
    (\Cref{thm:candidatefails}, \Cref{cor:localobstruction},
    \Cref{cor:cmdichotomy});
  \item a secant object of rank one whose support is a complete intersection
    of two divisors, for every $b$ and every $d$ (\Cref{thm:noci});
  \item a secant object quasi-isomorphic to a two-term complex of sums of
    powers of the polarisation, or of simple semi-homogeneous bundles, in
    particular one whose support has a Hilbert--Burch resolution by such
    bundles, in every dimension $n\ge3$ and for every rank, twist and
    discriminant (\Cref{thm:nosplit}, \Cref{thm:noblocks},
    \Cref{cor:disjointroutes});
  \item the semiregularity form of the propagation statement, for any
    representative of the Weil $K$-theory class that is a locally free sheaf
    on a smooth germ of codimension at least two of its support, at every
    member with no abelian subvariety tangent to a $K$-eigenspace. For every
    representative, injectivity of the semiregularity map forces the
    evaluation map to vanish on the
    $4n^{2}$-dimensional annihilator of the Weil class, which is the mixed
    part of a splitting $HH^{1}(A)=P\oplus Q$ into the annihilators of the two
    conjugate pieces of that class, so that the whole Hochschild action
    factors through a ring of dimension $2^{2n+1}-1$ inside one of dimension
    $2^{4n}$ (\Cref{thm:p2forces}, \Cref{cor:p2shape},
    \Cref{thm:hhsplitting}, \Cref{cor:hhfactor});
  \item the same form of the propagation statement for any representative
    whose support has codimension at least $n$, at every member of every
    family whatever its endomorphisms, hence for every complex carried by a
    cycle that represents the Weil class; and, at a member with no abelian
    subvariety tangent to a $K$-eigenspace, for any representative with a
    component of codimension at least two along which its cohomology has
    nonzero alternating length: two independent jet classes on a complex of
    finite length multiply to a class that the semiregularity map of a torus
    detects through the Euler characteristic, and the Weil line misses every
    class pulled back from a quotient by an abelian subvariety tangent to an
    eigenspace (\Cref{lem:finiteproduct}, \Cref{thm:p2support});
  \item the bounded form of the propagation statement read on the
    transported cycle, for every base cycle with a component of finite
    stabiliser, hence for every cycle this paper writes down: the transport
    multiplies the degree of a component by $c^{2n}/|\ker\varphi\cap G(Z)|$,
    and the denominator is bounded while the numerator is not
    (\Cref{lem:multiplicity}, \Cref{thm:p1forces}); and the same form read on
    a representative chosen afresh at each point, which is equivalent to the
    conjecture for the family and so carries no information
    (\Cref{thm:p1equivalent});
  \item at a quartic CM field, the weakened criterion for any complex with
    secant character that is, at some point, locally free on a smooth curve
    or on a point, or the ideal of a curve in a smooth divisor in general
    position with respect to the real multiplication, and for its Orlov
    products; in particular the explicit pair of
    \cite[Example~11.2.7]{Mar25c}, whose curves are glued in to correct the
    character, fails it at the points of those curves
    (\Cref{thm:quarticlocal}, \Cref{cor:markmanquarticfails}).
  \end{enumerate}
\item \emph{Every CM field, not only the quadratic ones.} For every CM field
  $F$ of degree $2m$, every $n\ge2$ and every discriminant, the family of
  abelian varieties of $(F,n,\delta)$-Weil type has a connected period domain
  of dimension $mn^{2}$ carrying a flat space of Weil classes of rank $2m$; it
  contains a member, isogenous to a product of CM abelian $m$-folds, at which
  those classes are polynomials with rational coefficients in divisor classes,
  hence algebraic (\Cref{thm:cmbasepoint}); the algebraic locus is stable
  under the rational points of $\Res_{F_{0}/\QQ}\SU(V,H)$, whose orbits are
  dense, and is a countable union of Zariski closed sets, hence either the
  whole domain or meagre (\Cref{thm:main}); and each of the two criteria of
  (iv) closes the whole family (\Cref{thm:cmpropagation}). The
  Weil classes of CM fields of higher degree are therefore not a separate
  problem: they pose the same propagation statement, with the base case
  supplied (\Cref{prop:cmweil}, \Cref{prop:composite}). The families are also
  linked: for each CM field the split families in infinitely many dimensions
  give all of its families, and the fields of degree at least four give the
  imaginary quadratic ones (\Cref{prop:descent}).
\item \emph{Exactly what remains.} The Hodge conjecture follows from the
  results of this paper together with three further statements: the
  propagation statement of (iv), in its corrected form and read for the split
  families of the CM fields of degree at least four, which by
  \Cref{prop:descent} carry the Weil classes of every CM field with them; the
  algebraicity of the Hodge classes on abelian varieties that divisor classes
  and Weil classes do not generate; and the conjecture for the varieties that
  are not abelian. The last of these is the conjecture itself, since it
  covers $A\times\PP^{1}$ for every abelian variety $A$ and so contains the
  abelian case. Its weaker form, the conjecture modulo the images of Hodge
  classes of abelian varieties under algebraic correspondences, holds on
  every variety whose cohomology is reached from abelian varieties in that
  way, on every variety of dimension $m$ in degrees $0$, $2$, $2m-2$ and
  $2m$, on every variety of dimension at most three, and on every fourfold
  and fivefold whose zero-cycles are supported in dimension at most three or
  whose maximal rationally connected quotient is birational to a variety on
  which it holds in degree four, on every fourfold and fivefold dominated by
  a rational map from such a variety, and on every smooth Delsarte
  hypersurface of every dimension; in general it is open. Over the rule set
  formed by the implications of the paper and of the literature there are
  exactly five minimal sufficient sets of open statements: the conjecture for
  the varieties that are not abelian, alone; the Lefschetz standard
  conjecture for every variety together with the motivatedness of every
  Hodge class; and the weaker form together with the Lefschetz standard
  conjecture, with the variational Hodge conjecture for algebraic classes, or
  with the propagation statement and the classes beyond the Weil lines, the
  last being the route of this paper.\footnote{These are the statements
  \textup{(L)}, \textup{(M)} and \textup{(V)} of \Cref{def:lmv}: $B(X)$ of
  \Cref{def:Bconj} for every smooth projective $X$; every Hodge class
  motivated in the sense of Andr\'e \cite[\S2.1]{And96b}; and, for every
  smooth projective morphism $f$ to a smooth connected base, a flat section of
  $R^{2p}f_{*}\QQ$ that is algebraic at one point is algebraic at every
  point.} The first two sets are each equivalent to the conjecture, every
  statement in the five sets other than the propagation statement is a
  consequence of it, and the secant construction of \Cref{sec:construction}
  lies outside all five (\Cref{prop:f3ishc}, \Cref{def:f3prime},
  \Cref{prop:f3prime}, \Cref{thm:closure}, \Cref{cor:fourremain},
  \Cref{prop:lefmot}, \Cref{prop:lefvhc}, \Cref{thm:vhcab}). The second and
  third of the three statements are not reductions to Weil classes or to
  abelian varieties: the self-product of a Mumford fourfold carries two Hodge
  classes outside the subring generated by divisor and Weil classes, and the
  $H^{3}$ of a very general quintic threefold is not of abelian type
  (\Cref{prop:mumfordproduct}, \Cref{prop:noabeliantype}). More is true of
  the second: it implies the conjecture for every abelian variety, so that
  with the third it already suffices, and the propagation statement is needed
  only for the Weil classes themselves (\Cref{prop:f2isab},
  \Cref{rem:f2rule}). Those two classes
  are the real multiplication by a totally real cubic field on the primitive
  part of $H^{2}$, and they are algebraic once the Kuga--Satake class of one
  K3 surface of Picard number thirteen attached to the fourfold is algebraic
  (\Cref{prop:mumfordrm}, \Cref{thm:mumfordks}). They are algebraic at every
  CM point, and they are algebraic on every member of a Mumford family
  exactly when the Lefschetz standard conjecture holds for the ninefold
  $W\times_{C}W$; likewise, the algebraicity of the Weil classes throughout
  one Weil family is equivalent to the Lefschetz standard conjecture for one
  total space
  (\Cref{thm:mumfordcm}, \Cref{cor:mumfordequiv}, \Cref{cor:weilequiv}). The
  Mumford classes are rigid, their Hodge locus being the compact base curve,
  and they are algebraic on every member as soon as one perfect complex at
  one CM point has $\Ext^{2}$ of dimension $119$, or as soon as they are
  represented at infinitely many CM points by cycles of bounded degree
  (\Cref{thm:mumfordrigid}, \Cref{thm:mumfordnumerical},
  \Cref{cor:mumfordbounded}). No complex with their Chern character is built
  from line bundles; for a Zariski dense set of those classes, such a complex
  with no negative self-extensions has self-extensions of total dimension at
  least $1600$; and such a complex extends along no Hochschild direction but
  the curve (\Cref{thm:mumfordprofile}, \Cref{prop:mumforddivisor},
  \Cref{cor:mumfordsplit}). No construction from line bundles by cones,
  tensor products, pull-backs, push-forwards and Fourier--Mukai functors
  reaches them at any point of the curve, and no twistor line through a
  member of the family keeps them of Hodge type. They are the twisted dual
  forms of the part of $H^{2}$ of the square generated by its symplectic
  class, and outside the CM points no rational map from the square to a K3
  surface or to a hyperk\"ahler eightfold pulls back a symplectic form to a
  nonzero form. At every closed point of every reduction of the curve modulo
  a prime they are represented by algebraic cycles, so that they are
  algebraic on every member exactly when those cycles can be chosen with
  bounded Hilbert data on a Zariski dense set of closed points
  (\Cref{thm:lefschetzclosure}, \Cref{prop:notwistor}, \Cref{prop:hksquare},
  \Cref{prop:modp}, \Cref{thm:modpdensity}). Of the routes to them that are
  not closed off, three are this one statement in other forms and two are
  stronger inputs, and any one of them would suffice
  (\Cref{cor:mumfordone}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each clause is the theorem or theorems cited with it, and no clause is used in
the proof of another. The computations cited as items are those of
\Cref{sec:verification}. For (i), \Cref{thm:hdgcount} counts the Hodge
classes, \Cref{cor:whatisleft} reduces every degree to $\omega_{1}$, and
\Cref{thm:explicitgens}(i) shows that $\omega_{1}$ has integer coefficients
and exactly $2^{2n-1}$ nonzero terms. For (ii), \Cref{thm:residual}(a) and
(b) are the two implications, and \Cref{prop:weildomain} gives the
connectedness and the dimension. For (iii), \Cref{thm:quatweil}(v) supplies
the cycle at a quaternionic point, and \Cref{thm:everyfamily} places such a
point in every family, by the discriminant computation of
\Cref{thm:quatweil}(iv) together with \Cref{lem:products}.

For (iv), the sufficient condition is \Cref{thm:semiregclosure} with
\Cref{rem:p2prime}. The refutation of its pure form is \Cref{thm:p2false},
which rests on \Cref{lem:weiltorushodge}, \Cref{lem:weiltorussubsets} and
\Cref{prop:weiltorussheaves} and on item (LI); the lower bound is
\Cref{cor:hhfactor}(i), the sufficiency of the minimum is
\Cref{thm:p2numerical}(iii), and the shape of the object is
\Cref{thm:p2endo}. For the corrected form, the number, the sufficiency of
attaining it and the excluded shapes are \Cref{thm:p2primenumber}(i), (iv)
and (v), and the Hodge locus of the other shapes is
\Cref{prop:p2primelocus}(iii) and (iv) with \Cref{rem:p2primeexceptional},
all with item (LII). The bounded criterion implies the conclusion by
\Cref{thm:degreebound}, is implied by it by \Cref{thm:p1equivalent}, and
fails on the transported cycle by \Cref{thm:p1forces}. For (v), each item is
the statement of the theorem cited, under its own hypotheses, which
\Cref{sec:scope} delimits item by item. For (vi), \Cref{prop:cmweil} gives
the family and the reduction to one class, \Cref{thm:cmbasepoint} the base
point and the representative, \Cref{thm:cmpropagation} the density and the
two criteria, and \Cref{prop:descent}, with item (LIV), the links between the
families.

For (vii), \Cref{prop:f3ishc} shows that the third statement is the
conjecture, and \Cref{prop:f3prime}, \Cref{prop:f3primesmall} and
\Cref{prop:f3primefibration} give what is known of its weaker form.
\Cref{thm:closure}(ii) and (iii) give the five minimal sets and the necessity
of each of their elements, \Cref{thm:closure}(vi) gives the consequences of
the conjecture, with \Cref{prop:lefmot}, \Cref{prop:lefvhc} and
\Cref{thm:vhcab} for the rules taken from the literature, and
\Cref{thm:closure}(iv) places the secant construction outside all five sets.
The computation is item (XXXIII), and Section 24 of the Lean certificate
checks it again. The remaining sentences of (vii), on the classes beyond the
Weil lines, are in order \Cref{prop:mumfordproduct} and
\Cref{prop:noabeliantype}, with item (XXXV); \Cref{prop:mumfordrm} and
\Cref{thm:mumfordks}(ii), with item (XLI); \Cref{thm:mumfordcm},
\Cref{cor:mumfordequiv} and \Cref{cor:weilequiv}, with item (XLIII);
\Cref{thm:mumfordrigid}, \Cref{thm:mumfordnumerical},
\Cref{cor:mumfordbounded}, \Cref{thm:mumfordprofile},
\Cref{prop:mumforddivisor} and \Cref{cor:mumfordsplit}, with items (XLIV) and
(XLV) and two theorems of the Lean certificate (\Cref{sec:lean});
\Cref{thm:lefschetzclosure}, with item (XLVI) and a third theorem of the
certificate; \Cref{prop:notwistor}, with item (XLVII); \Cref{prop:hksquare},
with item (XLVIII); \Cref{prop:modp} and \Cref{thm:modpdensity}, which rest
on \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Li26}; and \Cref{cor:mumfordone}, with item (XLIX).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What this closes]\label{rem:whatclosure}
\Cref{thm:final} settles four things.

The problem has been brought to its smallest form. By (i) and (ii) the Hodge
conjecture for a whole family of abelian varieties of Weil type is the
algebraicity of one explicitly written class at every point of one connected
domain, and the reduction loses nothing, since the converse holds as well.

The base case is not the difficulty. By (iii) every family has a base point,
for every field, every dimension and every discriminant, and the cycle there
is a polynomial in two divisor classes rather than an existence statement.
Nor is the discriminant the difficulty: by \Cref{prop:descent} the split
families in all dimensions give every discriminant, and the fields of degree
at least four give the imaginary quadratic ones, so neither the base points
of nontrivial discriminant nor those of the quadratic fields carry weight in
any minimal derivation. What separates a base point from the family is
propagation.

Propagation has one genuine criterion, and in its pure form that criterion
cannot be met. The bounded criterion is equivalent to its own conclusion, so
it restates the problem rather than reducing it. The semiregularity criterion
would be met by one perfect complex at one base point with $\Ext^{2}$ of
dimension $2n(2n-1)$, but no complex whose Chern character is exactly a Weil
class is semiregular, because semiregularity would carry it to non-algebraic
Weil tori, on which the Weil class is a Hodge class but not a Chern class.
The criterion survives only with powers of the polarisation added to the
Chern character, and in that form it is again one number:
\Cref{thm:p2primenumber} computes the rank that $\dim\Ext^{2}(E,E)$ must
reach, $(4+\rho)n^{2}-2n$ for $n\ge3$ with $\rho$ the rank of a Hankel matrix
of the coefficients, and shows that an object reaching it satisfies the
criterion. No finite direct sum of shifted push-forwards of line bundles
along isogenies, the simple semi-homogeneous bundles among them, meets the
criterion (\Cref{prop:scalaratiyah}). Injectivity is also more than
propagation needs, since a flatness condition on the deformations of the
object suffices (\Cref{prop:p2flat}). None of this decides whether an object
meeting either condition exists.

The routes that fail are known, and they fail for identifiable structural
causes rather than by exhaustion of cases. In (v), a divisor-generated Chern
character cannot leave the divisor subring; a norm
eigenclass sits in the wrong summand of the character grading; a Chern
character on the Weil line makes the annihilator too large by exactly the
dimension of the family; a support that is not Cohen--Macaulay carries a
local $\cE xt^{2}$ that the compensating scalar cannot meet; a two-term
complex of powers of the polarisation has summand inclusions that
$H^{2}(\cO)$ does not kill; and a component of nonzero multiplicity makes two
transverse translation classes multiply to a class that the Euler
characteristic detects; and at a quartic field a curve along which the object
is locally free is tangent to at most one of the two eigenplanes, so the
square of the Atiyah class along the other one survives. A more careful version of the same construction would
not avoid these obstacles.

What is not settled is the conjecture, and (vii) states exactly how far from
it these results stand. The route of this paper passes through three
statements. Taken as the conjecture for all varieties that are not abelian,
the third is the conjecture itself (\Cref{prop:f3ishc}); weakened to the
conjecture modulo abelian varieties, it makes the three a minimal sufficient
set over the rule set of \Cref{thm:closure}, which does not contain the
implication of \Cref{prop:f2isab}; with that implication the second and
third already suffice. They are then the propagation statement in the corrected form of (iv),
read for the split families of the CM fields of degree at least four, which
by \Cref{prop:descent} carry the Weil classes of every CM field with them;
the conjecture for the Hodge classes on abelian varieties that no Weil line
reaches, which does not reduce to Weil classes and is equivalent to the
conjecture for all abelian varieties; and the conjecture modulo
abelian varieties, which holds wherever the cohomology is reached from
abelian varieties by algebraic correspondences and on the fourfolds and
fivefolds whose zero-cycles are supported in dimension at most three or
whose maximal rationally connected quotient satisfies it in degree four,
descends in degree four along dominant rational maps, holds on the smooth
Delsarte hypersurfaces of every dimension, and is open in general
(\Cref{prop:f3prime}, \Cref{prop:f3primesmall},
\Cref{prop:f3primefibration}, \Cref{prop:f3primedominant},
\Cref{cor:delsarteall}, \Cref{rem:f3primeopen}). The shorter routes of
the literature replace the first two by the Lefschetz standard conjecture or
the variational Hodge conjecture, and the Lefschetz standard conjecture is
the statement at their centre (\Cref{rem:bullseye}). The smallest case of the
second statement follows from the algebraicity of a single correspondence,
the Kuga--Satake class of one K3 surface of Picard number thirteen, which
amounts to that case together with a link (\Cref{prop:mumfordkslink}). A
standard open case of the third, the square of a K3 surface with real
multiplication, meets the same kind of correspondence, the Kuga--Satake
correspondence of that surface. That is where a new source of algebraic
cycles would have to act first.

We prove none of the three statements, nor the Lefschetz standard conjecture
beyond the total spaces of the abelian schemes over curves of
\Cref{cor:mumfordlefschetz} and \Cref{cor:lefschetzsmall}, nor the
motivatedness of Hodge classes, nor the variational Hodge conjecture. What
(vii) adds is that this boundary is the outcome of a computation over a
finite rule set, not a judgement, and so it can be checked rather than taken
on trust.
\end{remark}
