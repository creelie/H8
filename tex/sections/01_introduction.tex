\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Let $X$ be a smooth complex projective variety. The Hodge conjecture asserts
that the cycle class map
\[
  \cl^{p}_{X}\colon \CH^{p}(X)_{\QQ}\longrightarrow \Hdg^{p}(X)
  \coloneqq H^{2p}(X,\QQ)\cap H^{p,p}(X)
\]
is surjective for every $p$. For $p=1$ this is the theorem of Lefschetz on
$(1,1)$ classes.%
\footnote{The rational coefficients are essential. The theorem of Lefschetz
holds integrally in degree two, but the integral analogue fails in higher
degree: Atiyah and Hirzebruch found torsion classes of type $(p,p)$ that are
not classes of cycles, and Koll\'ar found non-torsion ones. Every statement in
this paper concerns $\QQ$-coefficients.}
For $p\ge2$ the conjecture is open, and two lines of attack on it are tried
most often.

The first is algebraic and runs through abelian varieties. By the structure
theory of Hodge classes on abelian varieties, the Hodge ring of an abelian
variety of Weil type is generated by divisor classes together with finitely
many exceptional classes in middle degree, the Weil classes of
\cite{Weil77}.%
\footnote{Weil exhibited these classes as Hodge classes outside the subring
generated by divisor classes and proposed them as test cases for the Hodge
conjecture; they have served as the standard test case since.}
Divisor classes are algebraic, so for these varieties the question is whether
the Weil classes are algebraic. Markman \cite{Mar25a,Mar25b} proved that they
are algebraic on the abelian sixfolds of Weil type of trivial discriminant,%
\footnote{In the normalisation of \Cref{def:disc} the discriminant is the
class of $(-1)^{n}\det H$ in $\QQ^{\times}_{>0}/\Nm_{K/\QQ}(K^{\times})$.
Markman uses the class of $\det H$ itself, in which the same sixfolds have
discriminant $(-1)^{3}=-1$.} which are the sixfolds of split Weil type, for
every imaginary quadratic field, and deduced it for all abelian fourfolds of
Weil type, for every field and every discriminant, through a degeneration
argument of Schoen. The Hodge conjecture for abelian fourfolds follows, and
Markman records the same conclusion for abelian fivefolds, through the
description of their Hodge rings by Moonen and Zarhin \cite{MZ99}. The sheaf
he produces has positive rank, is built from the geometry of the abelian
variety itself through a pair of secant sheaves on an abelian threefold, and
satisfies an equivariant semiregularity condition.%
\footnote{This condition is stronger than injectivity of the semiregularity
map. Markman asks in \cite[Question~11.4]{Mar25b} whether it can be relaxed
to injectivity on the image of an evaluation map, and notes in his
Section~12 that an affirmative answer would carry the method to abelian
varieties of dimension at least eight and to fields of larger degree. The
relaxed hypothesis fails for the objects whose Chern character is
concentrated on the Weil line and on which $H^{2}$ of the structure sheaf
acts faithfully (\Cref{thm:weakfails}), for every object with a
line bundle summand off the polarisation line (\Cref{thm:linebundle},
\Cref{cor:noline}), and for Markman's own candidate in dimension eight
(\Cref{thm:candidatefails}).}
For abelian $2n$-folds of Weil type with $n\ge4$, and with $n=3$ and
nontrivial discriminant, the question is open. A natural plan for these cases
is to import an object from an ambient projective space, restrict it to the
dual abelian variety, and transport the result back by the Fourier--Mukai
transform. Secant varieties of the dual are the usual choice of ambient
object, because a dimension count makes the codimension come out right.

The second attack is geometric and runs through induction on dimension. One
places the variety in a family of hyperplane sections, uses the algebraicity
of the Hodge locus \cite{CDK95} to control where the class stays of type
$(p,p)$, appeals to the conjecture in one dimension lower on the sections, and
attempts to transport the resulting cycles back up. Each ingredient is a
theorem, and the question is whether they compose.

We show that neither attack can succeed, and that the reasons are different
in kind. The first fails for an algebraic reason that no refinement of the
construction can repair: the classes the method produces lie in the wrong
subring, and they lie there for reasons of $\Kx$-equivariance that are visible
before any geometry is chosen. The second fails for a cohomological reason
that is elementary once stated: the restriction map annihilates the classes
the induction would need to carry.

The rest of the paper concerns what remains once these obstructions are in
place. Every family of abelian varieties of Weil type, for every CM field,
every dimension and every discriminant, contains an explicit member at which
the Weil classes are polynomials in divisor classes (\Cref{thm:everyfamily},
\Cref{thm:cmbasepoint}), and the algebraicity of the Weil classes throughout
the family is equivalent to that of one class at every point of one connected
period domain, which gives the Hodge conjecture for every member with full
Hodge group (\Cref{cor:exactform}, \Cref{thm:cmpropagation}). What is missing
is propagation from the base point, and we determine what propagation
requires. The semiregularity criterion for it is never met in its pure form
(\Cref{thm:p2false}); in its corrected form it asks for one perfect complex at
one base point whose $\Ext^{2}$ reaches a dimension that we compute in closed
form (\Cref{thm:p2primenumber}). Finally, by taking consequences in a finite
set of implications, we identify the open statements that separate these
results from the Hodge conjecture (\Cref{thm:closure}). The Hodge conjecture
is not proved here; \Cref{sec:final} states what is proved and names what
remains.

\subsection{The algebraic obstruction}

Throughout, $A$ is a complex abelian variety of dimension $g$, $\Av$ is its
dual, $\cP$ is the Poincar\'e bundle on $A\times\Av$, and
$\Phi_{\cP}\colon D^{b}(\Av)\to D^{b}(A)$ is the Fourier--Mukai transform with
kernel $\cP$. We write $\theta$ for a principal polarisation class on $A$ and
$\theta'$ for the dual class on $\Av$.

\begin{theorem}[Divisor obstruction]\label{thm:divisor}
Let $A$ be a principally polarised complex abelian variety of dimension $g$,
let $i\colon\Av\to\PP^{N-1}$ be any morphism defined by a linear system, and
let $\cF$ be any object of $D^{b}(\PP^{N-1})$. Put
$\cE=\Phi_{\cP}(Li^{*}\cF)$. Then
\[
  \ch_{k}(\cE)\in\QQ\cdot\theta^{k}\qquad\text{for every }k\ge0 .
\]
\end{theorem}

The proof has two ingredients and is short. The Chern character commutes with
derived pullback, which confines $\ch(Li^{*}\cF)$ to the subring of
$H^{*}(\Av,\QQ)$ generated by the hyperplane class, and the hyperplane class of
a linear system on an abelian variety is a multiple of $\theta'$. The
cohomological Fourier--Mukai transform then carries each power of $\theta'$ to
a rational multiple of a power of $\theta$; we prove this in closed form in
\Cref{thm:fmtheta}. Neither ingredient uses anything about $\cF$.

Since a Weil class is by construction not in the subring generated by divisor
classes, the following is immediate.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:neverweil}
With notation as in \Cref{thm:divisor}, suppose in addition that $g=2n$ and
that $A$ is of $(K,-1,n)$-Weil type. Then $\ch_{n}(\cE)$ is a Weil class only
if $\ch_{n}(\cE)=0$.
\end{corollary}

A second proof of \Cref{cor:neverweil}, which does not pass through
\Cref{thm:divisor}, explains in terms of representations why the first had to
succeed. The endomorphism $\iota(\tau)$ attached to $\tau\in\Kx$ acts on
$H^{2n}(A,\QQ)$, and the $\Kx$-isotypic decomposition of that space separates
the two objects in question.

\begin{theorem}[Character obstruction]\label{thm:character}
Let $(A,\iota,\eta)$ be of $(K,-1,n)$-Weil type and write
$H^{1}(A,\QQ)\otimes\CC=V_{+}\oplus V_{-}$ for the decomposition into
eigenspaces of $\iota(\sqrt{-d})$. Then
\[
  H^{2n}(A,\QQ)\otimes\CC
  =\bigoplus_{r+s=2n}\textstyle\bigwedge^{r}V_{+}\otimes\bigwedge^{s}V_{-},
\]
with $\iota(\tau)$ acting on the $(r,s)$ summand by
$\tau^{r}\bbar{\tau}^{\,s}$.
The Weil line $\HW(A)$ spans the two extreme summands $(r,s)=(2n,0)$ and
$(0,2n)$, on which $\Kx$ acts by $\tau^{2n}$ and $\bbar{\tau}^{2n}$, while
$\eta^{n}$ lies in the middle summand $(r,s)=(n,n)$, on which $\Kx$ acts by
the norm character $\Nm_{K/\QQ}(\tau)^{n}$. In particular, for every $n\ge1$,
\[
  \HW(A)\cap H^{2n}(A,\QQ)^{\Nm^{n}}=0,
\]
so a class that is a $\Kx$-eigenclass for $\Nm^{n}$ has zero component along
the Weil line.
\end{theorem}

\Cref{thm:character} shows that the obstruction is structural. A construction
that is equivariant for the $K$-action returns an eigenclass, so it must choose
a character in advance, and the norm character is the one that the
polarisation already occupies. The Weil line sits at the two ends of the
$\Kx$-grading, as far from the middle as the grading allows;
\Cref{fig:charactergrid} draws the grading and marks the two positions.

There is no third obstruction of a numerical kind, and \Cref{sec:numerology}
explains why. A secant numerology producing a class of codimension $n$ on an
abelian variety of dimension $2n$ requires a polarisation on $\Av$ of Euler
characteristic $2n^{2}+2n$, which for $n\ge2$ is smaller than $2^{2n}$ and
much smaller than $3^{2n}$. These are the Euler characteristics at which the
theorem of Lefschetz guarantees that a linear system is free of base points
and that it is very ample, and it is tempting to read them as thresholds below
which no such system exists. Both are sufficient conditions and neither is
necessary: an abelian surface with a polarisation of type $(1,3)$ has
$\chi=3<2^{2}$ and a base-point-free system, and one with a polarisation of
type $(1,5)$ has $\chi=5<3^{2}$ and a very ample system, the latter being the
Horrocks--Mumford embedding in $\PP^{4}$ \cite{HM73,GP98}. The threshold
reading appears in the authors' earlier preprint cited on the title page, and
\Cref{sec:numerology} corrects it. Nothing else in the paper depends on it:
\Cref{thm:divisor,thm:character} use no numerical count.

\subsection{The geometric obstruction}

The second attack concerns induction on dimension. Let
$j\colon Y\hookrightarrow X$ be a smooth ample hypersurface in a smooth
projective variety of dimension $d$, and let $L=c_{1}(\cO_{X}(Y))$.

\begin{theorem}[Hyperplane obstruction]\label{thm:hyperplane}
Let $d=2p$ and let $\alpha\in H^{2p}(X,\QQ)$ be primitive. Then
$j^{*}\alpha=0$ for every smooth ample hypersurface $j\colon Y\hookrightarrow X$.
\end{theorem}

The proof, given in \Cref{sec:hyperplane}, takes three lines from the
projection formula and the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem. The primitive middle
degree is where the Hodge conjecture is hard, and it is where the restriction
map returns nothing. Below the middle degree the restriction map is injective,
and the difficulty moves to the return trip.

\begin{theorem}[The return trip]\label{thm:returntrip}
Let $2p\le d-2$, so that $j^{*}\colon H^{2p}(X,\QQ)\to H^{2p}(Y,\QQ)$ is an
isomorphism. If $j^{*}\alpha$ is algebraic on $Y$, then $L\alpha$ is algebraic
on $X$. The implication ``$L\alpha$ algebraic $\Rightarrow$ $\alpha$
algebraic'', asked of all smooth projective $X$ and all $\alpha$, is
equivalent to the Lefschetz standard conjecture.
\end{theorem}

Together the two theorems give the following.

\begin{corollary}[No dimensional induction through hyperplane sections]%
\label{cor:noinduction}
Let $X$ be smooth projective of dimension $d$ and let $\alpha\in\Hdg^{p}(X)$.
An argument that deduces the algebraicity of $\alpha$ from the Hodge
conjecture for smooth ample hypersurfaces of $X$, using only $j^{*}$ and
$j_{*}$, returns no information when $2p=d$ and $\alpha$ is primitive, and
otherwise establishes the algebraicity of $L\alpha$ rather than of $\alpha$.
\end{corollary}

\Cref{sec:reduction} then records what is left. By hard Lefschetz every Hodge
class is determined by primitive classes in degrees at most $d$, and the
Lefschetz standard conjecture is the obstruction to moving between degrees
algebraically. The outcome is an equivalence, \Cref{thm:equivalence}: the
Hodge conjecture holds for all smooth complex projective varieties if and only
if it holds for primitive classes of middle degree and the Lefschetz standard
conjecture holds. Neither side is easier than the other, so this is a
restatement; its use is that it isolates the primitive middle degree as the
place where new ideas have to enter, and \Cref{thm:hyperplane} shows that the
classical inductive handle is blind there.

\subsection{Scope of the obstructions}\label{ssec:introscope}

\Cref{sec:scope} draws the boundary of each obstruction, since an obstruction
theorem is useful only when its edges are sharp. Three of those boundaries
shape the rest of the paper.

\Cref{thm:divisor} does not apply to Markman's construction
\cite{Mar25a,Mar25b}, and \Cref{sec:escape} explains why, in a form meant for
anyone who wants to build a new construction. A rational class in the Weil
line has a $\Kx$-orbit spanning a plane, not a line (\Cref{prop:orbitplane}),
so any construction reaching it must be bilinear in a pair of inputs
interchanged by $\Gal(K/\QQ)$ (\Cref{cor:orbitcriterion}). Markman's
construction is a secant construction, and it meets this requirement: it
sites the secant geometry on an abelian $n$-fold $X$, attaches two conjugate
secant sheaves to the two points where a plane of Hodge classes meets the
spinor variety, and pairs them on $X\times\widehat{X}$. So the secant idea is
sound; what fails is siting it on $\Av$ inside a projective space, and
\Cref{prop:whichescaped} records the three independent ways in which that
fails. \Cref{q:higher} states what a construction above the known range would
have to supply, and \Cref{sec:construction} supplies three of its four
ingredients in closed form for every $n$ and every $K$. The abelian $2n$-fold
of Weil type is $X\times\widehat{X}$ with a $K$-action written down by one
element of $\NS(X)$ (\Cref{thm:explicitweil}), and the plane is
$P_{t}=\QQ u_{t}\oplus\QQ v_{t}$ with $u_{t}$ and $v_{t}$ the even and odd
parts of $e^{\sqrt{-d}\,t}$ (\Cref{thm:plane}). The third ingredient becomes
an explicit equation: a secant sheaf has rank $a$, $c_{1}=bt$,
$c_{2}=\tfrac{ad+b^{2}}{2}t^{2}$, and Bogomolov discriminant equal to the norm
form $\Nm_{K/\QQ}(b+a\sqrt{-d})$ times $t^{2}$
(\Cref{thm:secantinvariants}), so the numerical obstruction vanishes
identically. At $n=3$ the equation forces the sheaf to be
$\mathcal{I}_{Z}(\Theta)$ with $Z$ a union of $d+1$ translates of the
Abel--Jacobi curve of a genus three Jacobian (\Cref{prop:genus3}), which is
the shape of the object used in \cite{Mar25b}. The equation is solved in every
dimension. Since the secant plane has no preferred scale, one may pass to a
positive multiple of a rational point of it, and after clearing denominators
the Chern characters of the powers of a single line bundle span the subring
$\QQ[t]$, the matrix that expresses them in the basis $t^{k}/k!$ being a
Vandermonde matrix. A class of positive rank on a smooth projective variety is
the class of a coherent sheaf, so the sheaf exists (\Cref{lem:posrank},
\Cref{thm:secantexists}), with an explicit integer witness in line bundles: at
$n=4$, $d=3$ and $a=b=1$ the multiple is $6$ and the witness is
$-23[\cO_{X}]+66[\cO_{X}(t)]-54[\cO_{X}(2t)]+20[\cO_{X}(3t)]-3[\cO_{X}(4t)]$.
The fourth ingredient, the deformation argument, is not supplied
(\Cref{rem:deformation}), so none of this proves the Hodge conjecture for
$n\ge4$; \Cref{rem:thirdclosed} separates what is settled from what is not.

\Cref{thm:character} applies to every construction that is $K$-equivariant
with the norm character. A construction equivariant for the character
$\tau\mapsto\tau^{2n}$ would land in the right eigenspace, and
\Cref{rem:rightcharacter} records what such a construction would have to look
like. None is known. The difficulty is that $\tau\mapsto\tau^{2n}$ is not
$\QQ$-valued, so it cannot be realised by a correspondence defined over $\QQ$
without additional structure.

\Cref{thm:hyperplane} concerns hyperplane sections. It does not obstruct
induction through other families, for instance through degenerations, through
blow-ups, or through the Kuga--Satake correspondence, which changes the
variety instead of cutting it. \Cref{sec:ks} sets out that construction in
full and marks its boundary. For a weight two Hodge structure of K3 type it
transports the question to an abelian variety and turns the class into an
endomorphism, and \Cref{prop:ksnotrestriction} shows that
\Cref{thm:hyperplane} has no bearing on it. On an abelian variety of Weil
type, however, the hypothesis $h^{2,0}=1$ holds only for $n=1$
(\Cref{prop:normpart}), and $n=1$ is the case in which the Weil line already
consists of divisor classes.

\subsection{Explicit Weil classes and base points}

The obstructions concern a class that can be written down, and the positive
results of the paper begin there. \Cref{thm:explicitgens} writes the Weil
classes out. On the standard model of an abelian variety of $(K,-1,n)$-Weil
type, built in \Cref{sec:explicit} from a $K$-vector space of dimension $2n$
and a hermitian form of signature $(n,n)$, the Weil line has the integral
basis
\[
  \omega_{1}=\sum_{|T|\ \mathrm{even}}(-1)^{|T|/2}d^{\,n-|T|/2}\,w^{(T)},
  \qquad
  \omega_{2}=\sum_{|T|\ \mathrm{odd}}(-1)^{(|T|-1)/2}d^{\,n-(|T|+1)/2}\,w^{(T)},
\]
where $T$ runs over the subsets of $\{1,\dots,2n\}$ and $w^{(T)}$ is the
monomial taking $y_{j}$ for $j\in T$ and $x_{j}$ otherwise. Each has
$2^{2n-1}$ terms. In this form two things are visible at once: the monomials
occurring in them are disjoint from those occurring in the powers of the
polarisation, which gives an elementary second proof of \Cref{prop:notpoly};
and the balanced signature in the definition of Weil type is forced, since for
any other signature the same two classes are of type $(p,2n-p)$ and are not
Hodge classes.

\Cref{thm:hdgcount} counts the Hodge classes. For $A$ of $(K,-1,n)$-Weil type
with $\Hg(A)=\SU(V,H)$,
\[
  \dim_{\QQ}\Hdg^{k}(A)=\begin{cases}3,&k=n,\\1,&k\ne n,\end{cases}
  \qquad
  \Hdg^{n}(A)=\QQ\,\eta^{n}\oplus\HW(A),
\]
so the Hodge conjecture for such an $A$, in every degree at once, reduces to
the algebraicity of the single class $\omega_{1}$ (\Cref{cor:whatisleft}). The
obstruction theorems are therefore about one explicitly written class and not
about a space of unknown size.

\Cref{thm:divcriterion} produces that class from divisors whenever a certain
condition holds. Write $R$ for the rational structure on
$\bigwedge^{2}V_{+}\oplus\bigwedge^{2}V_{-}$, the part of $H^{2}(A,\QQ)$ on
which $\Kx$ acts through $\tau^{2}$ and $\bbar{\tau}^{\,2}$. If $R$ contains a
Hodge class whose component in $\bigwedge^{2}V_{-}$ is a nondegenerate form
$\Theta$, then $\HW(A)$ is spanned by the real and imaginary parts of
$\Theta^{n}$, and these are polynomials in two divisor classes, hence
algebraic. On $A=X\times\widehat{X}$ with the $K$-action of
\Cref{sec:construction} the condition holds with
$\Theta=d\beta-\widehat{\beta}-\sqrt{-d}\,\ell$ (\Cref{thm:splitclosed}). The
condition fails whenever $\Hg(A)$ is the full special unitary group
(\Cref{prop:divfails}), so the criterion is a Noether--Lefschetz condition on
$R$; and \Cref{prop:splitgeom} determines what the split members supply: they
have trivial discriminant, whatever polarisation is chosen, they form a locus
of codimension $n(n-1)/2$, and at such a point the three classes
$\beta,\widehat{\beta},\ell$ already exhaust both the N\'eron--Severi group
and the Hodge classes in the middle degree.

A Hodge class in $R$ is the same thing as an endomorphism $\mu$ with
$\mu\iota(\tau)=\iota(\bbar{\tau})\mu$ (\Cref{lem:Rendomorphism}), so the
criterion asks for a quaternion algebra $(-d,\mu^{2})_{\QQ}$ inside
$\End^{0}(A)$ extending $K$, and abelian varieties with such an algebra can be
written down.
For $B=(-d,b)_{\QQ}$ with $b>0$ acting on $B^{n}$, \Cref{thm:quatweil} shows
that the balanced signature is forced, computes the hermitian form as
$\mathrm{diag}(2a_{k}d,-2a_{k}bd)$, identifies the discriminant as the class
of $b^{n}$, and verifies the criterion with $\mu$ left multiplication by $j$.
Since $b$ may be any positive rational, and discriminants multiply over
products, \Cref{thm:everyfamily} follows: every Weil family, for every $K$,
every $n$ and every discriminant, contains a member whose Weil classes are
algebraic, with a representative that is a polynomial in two divisor classes.
What is left is propagation alone (\Cref{cor:R2}), and \Cref{cor:exactform}
puts it in its sharpest form. By Deligne's theorem \cite{Del82} the class
$\omega_{s}$ is absolutely Hodge at every point, so the Hodge conjecture for
the abelian varieties of Weil type with Hodge group $\SU(V,H)$, for every
imaginary quadratic field, every dimension and every discriminant, is
equivalent to the algebraicity of this one absolutely Hodge class at every
point of its family.

Products give base points of a second kind. If $A_{1}$ and $A_{2}$ are of Weil
type for the same $K$, with parameters $n_{1}$ and $n_{2}$, then
$A_{1}\times A_{2}$ is of Weil type with parameter $n_{1}+n_{2}$, and the Weil
class of the product is a fixed rational combination of external products of
the Weil classes of the factors (\Cref{thm:weilmult}). In terms of the
$K$-valued invariant $\omega=\omega_{1}+\sqrt{-d}\,\omega_{2}$ the combination
reads $\omega(A_{1})\omega(A_{2})=2\,\omega(A_{1}\times A_{2})$, so the Weil
class is multiplicative and algebraicity passes from the factors to the
product (\Cref{cor:prodalg}). At $n=1$ the Weil classes lie in $H^{2}$ and are
algebraic by the theorem of Lefschetz, so a product of $n$ abelian surfaces of
Weil type is, in every dimension and for every $d$, an abelian $2n$-fold of
Weil type whose Weil classes are an explicit integral polynomial in $2n$
divisor classes (\Cref{thm:everyn}). Like \Cref{cor:splitalgebraic} and
\Cref{thm:everyfamily}, this uses nothing beyond the Lefschetz theorem on
$(1,1)$ classes. Its reach is small: the locus of such products has dimension
$n$ inside a family of dimension $n^{2}$, smaller even than the quaternionic
locus, and a general member of the family is simple and lies on none of them
(\Cref{rem:prodscope}). Products move up in dimension; the descent lemma of
\Cref{sec:scope}, described below, moves down.

\subsection{Propagation}

Write $\Sigma\subseteq\cD_{n,n}$ for the set of points at which $\omega$ is
algebraic. \Cref{sec:propagation} first determines what kind of set it is.
Because the relative Hilbert scheme is proper and the cycle class is locally
constant in a flat family, the part of $\Sigma$ cut out by one tuple of
Hilbert polynomials is Zariski closed, and $\Sigma$ is the countable union of
those pieces (\Cref{thm:closedlocus}). Because algebraicity survives an
isogeny and $\SU(V,H)$ acts trivially on the Weil line, $\Sigma$ is stable
under $\SU(V,H)(\QQ)$; that group has dense orbits, so $\Sigma$ is dense
(\Cref{prop:densealg}). Together these give a dichotomy with nothing between
its halves: either $\Sigma$ is everything, and the Hodge conjecture holds for
all abelian varieties of Weil type in the family, or $\Sigma$ has empty
interior and the conjecture fails outside a meagre set
(\Cref{cor:allornothing}).

The dichotomy turns propagation into two statements of finite character.
Neither mentions a general point, and neither assumes the variational Hodge
conjecture: the passage from a formal deformation at one point to cycles
everywhere, which is the step usually missing, is supplied by
\Cref{thm:closedlocus}. The first asks for a bound (\Cref{thm:degreebound}):
if at every point of the rational orbit of a base point the Weil class is
represented by a cycle whose Hilbert data comes from one finite set, the
conjecture follows for the whole family; at $n=2$, where the quaternionic
locus is a divisor, infinitely many repetitions suffice rather than all but
finitely many (\Cref{rem:ntwo}). The leading coefficient of the Hilbert
polynomial is constant along the orbit, because $G(\QQ)$ preserves both the
Weil class and the polarisation, so only the degree of the effective part of a
representing cycle has to be bounded (\Cref{prop:leadingconstant},
\Cref{rem:whereunbounded}). The divisorial constructions cannot supply that
bound: products of effective divisors of bounded degree represent $\omega$
only on a nowhere dense set (\Cref{prop:divroute}), and \Cref{prop:pencil}
shows the degrees growing along an explicit pencil of quaternionic loci in one
fixed polarised lattice. The bound is, however, a restatement and not a route.
Read on an arbitrary representative it is equivalent to its conclusion
(\Cref{thm:p1equivalent}), and read on the transported cycle, the only
representative one can name, it is false: the transport multiplies the degree
of a component by $c^{2n}$ and divides it by the order of the stabiliser the
kernel meets, which is bounded for any component with finite stabiliser
(\Cref{lem:multiplicity}, \Cref{thm:p1forces}).

The second statement asks for a rank (\Cref{thm:semiregclosure}). The base
point carries a perfect complex whose Chern character is $N\omega_{1}$ in
degree $n$ and zero elsewhere (\Cref{prop:ktheory}), and if its
semiregularity map is injective, the conjecture again follows for the whole
family. That complex does not meet the criterion, and \Cref{sec:semireg}
shows why, in a form that applies to every object with the same Chern
character. The annihilator of the Weil line in $H^{0,2}$ has dimension $n^{2}$
(\Cref{lem:annihilator}), so an object whose Chern character is concentrated
on the Weil line is not semiregular as soon as $H^{2}(\cO_{A})$ acts on it
faithfully, as it does on every virtual sum of line bundles
(\Cref{thm:rankzero}, \Cref{prop:concentrated}, \Cref{rem:killsreduction}).
The annihilator is not an accident of the construction. The polarisation
carries the tangent space to the Weil family isomorphically onto it
(\Cref{thm:anntangent}), and a polarisation-preserving deformation kills the
whole Weil line if and only if it commutes with $K$ (\Cref{prop:firstorder}),
so the family is also the first-order Hodge locus of the class. The kernel
therefore contains a canonical copy of the tangent space to the very family
across which the class was to be transported (\Cref{cor:kernelfamily}), and
no choice of base point inside the family removes it. The same space lies in
the image of the evaluation map of
Hochschild cohomology, so the weakened criterion of
\cite[Question~11.4]{Mar25b} is unavailable for these objects too
(\Cref{thm:weakfails}); and by the infinitesimal rigidity of divisor classes,
an object with a line bundle summand off the polarisation line is obstructed
along the family in a direction the semiregularity map cannot see
(\Cref{thm:linebundle}), which removes every sum of line bundles in every
dimension (\Cref{cor:noline}). What these results leave is the indecomposable
case of positive rank for which Markman's question was asked
(\Cref{rem:whatmeasures}).

The rest of \Cref{sec:propagation} determines what a complex meeting the
criterion would have to look like. Injectivity forces the evaluation map of
Hochschild cohomology to vanish on the $4n^{2}$-dimensional annihilator of the
Weil class, and so forbids the representative to be a sheaf on a smooth germ
of codimension two of its support (\Cref{thm:p2forces}, \Cref{cor:p2shape}).
That annihilator has a shape: $HH^{1}(A)$ splits as $P\oplus Q$ into the
annihilators of the two conjugate pieces $\alpha_{\pm}$ of the Weil class,
each of dimension $2n$, and the annihilator in degree two is $P\wedge Q$
(\Cref{thm:hhsplitting}). A complex meeting the criterion would therefore have
its whole Hochschild action factoring through
$\bigwedge^{*}P\oplus_{\CC}\bigwedge^{*}Q$, of dimension $2^{2n+1}-1$ in a ring
of dimension $2^{4n}$: it would see the two halves of the class separately and
their interaction not at all. With no hypothesis on semiregularity, every
perfect complex with Chern character on the Weil line has
$\dim\Ext^{2}(E,E)\ge2n(2n-1)$ (\Cref{cor:hhfactor}), and a single complex at a
single base point attaining this minimum would have injective semiregularity
map and close the family (\Cref{thm:p2numerical}). Such a complex would have
$\chi(E,E)>0$ by the Hodge--Riemann relations and an indecomposable summand
carrying the class, and at $n=2$, and at $n=3$ under the compatibility of
\Cref{cor:hhfactor}(iii), that summand would have at least three independent
endomorphisms, so that no simple object could serve (\Cref{thm:p2endo}). Its
support is constrained as well. Two independent jet classes on a complex of
finite length multiply to a nonzero class whenever its Euler characteristic
is nonzero, because on a torus the semiregularity map sees that product
through the Euler characteristic (\Cref{lem:finiteproduct}). Applied on a
slice transverse to a component of the support, this shows that every
component of codimension at least two with nonzero multiplicity is a union of
cosets of an abelian subvariety tangent to a $K$-eigenspace, and since the
Weil line misses every class pulled back from such a quotient, the support
must have a component of codimension less than $n$ (\Cref{thm:p2support}). No
complex carried by a cycle that represents the Weil class can serve, at any
member of any family. The hypothesis on the Euler characteristic is sharp:
the cone of the product itself, on the Koszul complex of a point, has Euler
characteristic zero and kills it.

\subsection{The pure and corrected criteria}

These requirements cannot be met. \Cref{sec:corrected} proves that no
complex whose Chern character is a multiple of the Weil class, with no other
component, is semiregular (\Cref{thm:p2false}). Semiregularity would make it
extend along every $K$-linear deformation of the abelian variety, and those
deformations reach complex tori that are not algebraic. On a very general
such Weil torus the only Hodge classes strictly between degree zero and the
top degree are the Weil classes, the only subvarieties are points, and every
coherent sheaf has vanishing Chern character in degrees $1$ to $2n-1$. In
dimension four this is Voisin's theorem \cite{Voi02b}, and the same argument,
through the Hermite--Einstein metrics of Bando and Siu \cite{BS94}, gives it
in every dimension (\Cref{lem:weiltorushodge}, \Cref{lem:weiltorussubsets},
\Cref{prop:weiltorussheaves}). So the minimum $2n(2n-1)$ is attained by no
complex, and the descriptions of a complex meeting the pure criterion
(\Cref{thm:p2forces}, \Cref{thm:p2numerical}, \Cref{thm:p2endo},
\Cref{thm:p2support}) are true implications with no instance.
\Cref{rem:tworemain} records, for each of the two criteria, what it asks for
and in what form it is settled.

The criterion survives in a corrected form in which the complex may also
carry powers of the polarisation, which are Hodge classes on the polarised
family and not beyond it. The propagation theorem holds for the corrected
form unchanged, and apart from the shapes described below the corrected form
escapes the obstruction (\Cref{rem:p2prime}); the semiregular objects in
Markman's proofs are not of the pure form either. \Cref{thm:p2primenumber}
computes the corrected criterion in closed form. For a Chern character
$N\omega+\sum_{k}c_{k}\theta^{k}$ and $n\ge3$ the annihilator in $HT^{2}$ has
dimension $n^{2}(4-\rho)$, with $\rho\le3$ the rank of the Hankel matrix of the
numbers $k!\,c_{k}$,%
\footnote{That is, $\rho$ is the rank of the $(2n-1)\times3$ matrix with
entries $\mu_{i+j}$, $0\le j\le2n-2$, $0\le i\le2$, where $\mu_{k}=k!\,c_{k}$
(\Cref{setup:p2prime}). A vector in its kernel is a linear recurrence of order
at most two among the $\mu_{k}$, and $\rho$ does not change when the character
is multiplied by $e^{t\theta}$.}
and for a general shape the annihilator is the tangent space of the polarised
Weil family. So one complex with $\dim\Ext^{2}(E,E)=7n^{2}-2n$ at one base
point would close the family; the value is $24$ at $n=2$ and $57$ at $n=3$.
The shapes $Ae^{t\theta}+B\theta^{2n}$ with $t\theta$ the class of a line
bundle, of Hankel rank at most two, are excluded, by a twist and
\Cref{thm:p2false}. For every other shape and $n\ge3$ the Hodge locus of the
character is the polarised family itself, so the argument through
non-algebraic tori has nothing to act on (\Cref{prop:p2primelocus});%
\footnote{At $n=2$ the same holds with one exception: when $c_{1}=c_{3}=0$
and $c_{2}^{2}\int_{A}\theta^{4}=\tfrac34\int_{A}(N\omega)^{2}$ the locus has
dimension five, its stabiliser is $\mathfrak{so}(4,3)$, and it reaches tori
that are not algebraic; the argument of \Cref{thm:p2false} can be started
there but does not finish (\Cref{prop:p2primelocus}(iv),
\Cref{rem:p2primeexceptional}).}
and a weaker flatness form of the criterion suffices (\Cref{prop:p2flat}).
\Cref{prop:p2primeprofile} gives the Hochschild profile of a corrected
character.

The end of \Cref{sec:corrected} asks which objects can meet the corrected
criterion. No finite direct sum of shifted line bundles, or of the bundles
$\pi_{*}L$ pushed forward along isogenies, among them the simple
semi-homogeneous bundles, meets it (\Cref{prop:scalaratiyah}); the
corresponding statement for the pure form, \Cref{thm:nosum}, is vacuous by
\Cref{thm:p2false}. For the imaginary quadratic families the corrected
criterion is a statement about one indecomposable object, and no sum of
objects with exterior Ext algebras meets it (\Cref{lem:p2primesummands},
\Cref{cor:naturalsums}). The Orlov products of explicit secant objects meet
its twisted form with equality at $n=2$ and $n=3$, which gives the
injectivity of their semiregularity maps from a dimension count, and cannot
do so at $n=4$ when their negative Ext groups vanish (\Cref{thm:twistedcert},
\Cref{prop:orlovfour}). Their twisted characters are flat in every dimension
(\Cref{prop:flatall}), and equality in the count characterises the secant
objects that would make the template work, with negative Ext groups allowed
(\Cref{prop:orlovequality}); and at $n=4$ a
combination of natural objects with a Weil part needs at least eight of them,
on one conic of a quadric of Lagrangians (\Cref{thm:minimalsupport}). None of
this is a new case of the conjecture. The Mumford classes of
\Cref{sec:beyond} escape the obstruction of \Cref{thm:p2false} for a
different reason: they are rigid, and their Hodge locus is the Mumford curve.

\subsection{Secant objects}

\Cref{sec:secantobjects} examines the objects that a secant construction on
the split member can supply, beginning with the simplest. For a sum of line
bundles the semiregularity map is computed in closed form
(\Cref{lem:splitsemireg}): it annihilates every off-diagonal extension class
and the traceless part of every diagonal one, so injectivity forces
multiplicity one, a vanishing of cohomology that the shifts can arrange, and
the injectivity of $(\zeta_{i})\mapsto\sum_{i}e^{\lambda_{i}}\zeta_{i}$. The
Chern character condition then fails outright. By \Cref{thm:positivity}, at
any member whose only divisor classes of norm character are the multiples of
$\eta$, if the Chern character of an object is an effective combination
$\sum_{i}m_{i}e^{\lambda_{i}}$ of exponentials of divisor classes and
$\ch_{2}$ is a flat Hodge class, then the coefficient of
$\theta^{+}\theta^{-}$ is $\sum_{i}m_{i}\Nm_{K/\QQ}(u_{i})$ and must vanish;
the norm form is positive definite and the $m_{i}$ are positive, so every
$u_{i}$ is zero and the Weil component of the Chern character is zero as
well. The separation of $\theta^{+}\theta^{-}$ from $\eta^{2}$ that this needs
is a statement about tensor rank (\Cref{lem:tensorrank}): in the isotypic
component for the square of the norm character the first class has rank one
and the second has rank $\binom{2n}{2}$. The hypothesis on the Chern
character holds for every sheaf filtered by line bundles, for every
semi-homogeneous bundle in the sense of Mukai \cite{Muk78}, and for every
iterated extension of those; the hypothesis on the divisor classes holds at
every split member and at every quaternionic base point. So the candidates
one would reach for first do not exist, for a reason of positivity, and what
is left must be indecomposable in the derived category with a class in
$K_{0}$ that is not effective.

The arithmetic of the Chern character conditions gives an independent view of
the same candidates. When a sum of line bundles is indexed by elements $u_{i}$
of the ring of integers of $K$, the conditions become
$T_{q,r}=\sum_{i}m_{i}u_{i}^{q}\bbar u_{i}^{\,r}=0$ for all $q,r\le n$ but
three, and grouping the $u_{i}$ by norm turns the diagonal part of that system
into $\sum_{j}M_{j}N_{j}^{r}=0$ for $r=1,\dots,n$, where
$N_{1}<\dots<N_{\nu}$ are the norms that occur and $M_{j}$ is the sum of the
multiplicities at level $N_{j}$. Fewer than $n+1$ norms force every $M_{j}$ to
vanish and the rank with it (\Cref{cor:nplusone}); with $n+1$ norms the level
sums are forced by Lagrange interpolation, and since multiplicity one allows
each element of the ring only once, they are capped by the number of elements
of each norm. For $4\le n\le8$, six imaginary quadratic fields and norms up to
the bounds of item (XXII) of \Cref{sec:verification}, no set of norms meets
the two demands (\Cref{thm:pterange}). At $n=3$, with norms at most $200$,
eleven sets of four norms survive, all at $d=1$ or $d=2$; the smallest is
$(9,18,27,36)$ over $\QQ(\sqrt{-2})$, where the level sums are $(4,-6,4,-1)$
and the object would have rank one. For all eleven the rest of the system has
no solution, by an exhaustive search over sign patterns that covers
$4.29\times10^{14}$ cases in the largest instance. \Cref{cor:noline} removes
all of these objects at once, with no bound on the norms and for the weakened
criterion as well. On the explicit split object, \Cref{prop:splitobstruction}
computes the first-order obstruction against the family: its kernel is the
tangent space to the split locus, and its image lies in the kernel of the
semiregularity map and meets the copy of the tangent space that
\Cref{cor:kernelfamily} places there only in zero. \Cref{prop:evaluation}
writes out
the evaluation map of Hochschild cohomology on the same object and finds the
whole kernel of the semiregularity map in its image.

The one concrete candidate above $n=3$ is Markman's, in dimension eight
\cite[Section~12]{Mar25b}. \Cref{prop:markman8} computes its Chern character,
places it at the point $(1,3)$ of the secant plane, and reduces its
verification to one condition on one sheaf. \Cref{thm:factor} moves that
condition from the eightfold to the fourfold by computing the Hochschild
classes that preserve any secant Chern character, $n^{2}$ of them, so that
the weakened criterion for the transform of $\cF\boxtimes\cF$ becomes the
single identity $\mathrm{At}(\cF)^{2}=-d\Theta^{2}\cdot\id$ in
$\Ext^{2}(\cF,\cF\otimes\Omega^{2})$ for $\cF$ itself
(\Cref{cor:markmancondition}). \Cref{thm:candidatefails} decides that identity
in the negative, by a local computation at an isolated double point of the
support, so the candidate does not satisfy the weakened criterion, and
\Cref{cor:localobstruction} extends the obstruction to every dimension.

The local argument cannot reach a support without such a point, and
\Cref{ssec:smoothsupport} takes up that case. At a smooth point the sheaf
$\cE xt^{2}$ of the ideal vanishes, so both sides of the identity are locally
zero and a comparison of germs has nothing to compare
(\Cref{prop:smoothlocal}); the question moves from a point to the normal
bundle. In exchange the support, a surface $S$ in the fourfold, becomes
rigid. Since the Todd class of
an abelian variety is trivial, Grothendieck--Riemann--Roch turns the Chern
character into the whole numerical package of the support, and for
$\ch(\cF)=u_{\Theta}+b\,v_{\Theta}$ on a fourfold it gives
\[
  \chi(\cO_{S})=(b^{2}+d)(3b^{2}-d),\quad
  K_{S}^{2}=3(b^{2}+d)(7b^{2}-d),\quad
  e(S)=3(b^{2}+d)(5b^{2}-3d),
\]
with nothing left free. Because $\Omega^{1}_{S}$ is a quotient of the trivial
bundle $\Omega^{1}_{X}|_{S}$, it is globally generated, so $K_{S}$ is nef and
$e(S)\ge0$; then $K_{S}$ is also big, $S$ is minimal of general type, and the
Bogomolov--Miyaoka--Yau inequality applies. Its defect is
$3e(S)-K_{S}^{2}=24(b^{4}-d^{2})$, so the inequality says $d\le b^{2}$
(\Cref{thm:smoothinvariants}). At the value $b=3$ that the fourfold forces,
this leaves $d\in\{1,3,5,7\}$ (\Cref{cor:fourdiscriminants}): for every
imaginary quadratic field beyond those four there is no secant object of rank
one with smooth support, and for those four the invariants are the six
numbers of \Cref{tab:smoothsupport}.

What the local argument detects is a failure of depth. At any point where the
support is Cohen--Macaulay the sheaf $\cE xt^{2}$ of its ideal vanishes,
because by Auslander--Buchsbaum the ideal has projective dimension one there,
so both sides of the identity are locally zero (\Cref{thm:cmvanishing}). A
computation of these sheaves for ten germs (item (XXXI) of
\Cref{sec:verification}) confirms this and shows that it is sharp: the fat
plane and the cone over the twisted cubic are not complete intersections and
still carry no obstruction, while two planes meeting at a point, three planes
meeting pairwise at a point, and a plane with an embedded point all do. The
criterion therefore splits (\Cref{cor:cmdichotomy}): a support that is not
Cohen--Macaulay is removed outright, and a support that is Cohen--Macaulay has
no local obstruction at all, leaving one global vanishing in
$H^{1}(Z,\cE xt^{1}(\cI_{Z},\cI_{Z}))$. This also explains why Markman's
candidate cannot be repaired by normalising its double points: separating the
branches is what restores the depth, and it moves the Chern character off the
secant plane (\Cref{rem:normalisationcm}).

A Cohen--Macaulay support of codimension two has an ideal sheaf of projective
dimension one, by the same argument, so it is resolved by two vector bundles
of ranks $r+1$ and $r$, and \Cref{ssec:hilbertburch} examines that shape.
When the two bundles are sums of line bundles the Chern character condition
becomes four equations on the twists, and at $r=1$, the complete
intersections, they close outright for every $b$ and every $d$: weighting by
$w(x)=x^{2}+d$, which is positive because $d>0$, turns three of the equations
into the statement that two positive measures share their mass, mean and
variance, and a one-atom measure has variance zero, so the two divisor degrees
would have to coincide and the resolution would cancel (\Cref{thm:noci}). At
$r=2$ and $r=3$ an exact search over the twists finds nothing; at $r=4,5,6$
solutions appear, but only at $d=15$ and $d=23$ (\Cref{thm:burchsearch}).
None of them survives. A morphism from a secant object that satisfies the
criterion to a power of the polarisation is killed by $H^{2}(\cO)$
(\Cref{lem:annihilation}), and for the cone of a map between sums of such
powers the summand inclusions are not, because the relevant $H^{2}$ of a
nontrivial power vanishes; so no split resolution runs the criterion at any
rank, any twist or any discriminant (\Cref{thm:nosplit}), and the same holds
when the line bundles are replaced by simple semi-homogeneous bundles, the
bundles with scalar Atiyah class (\Cref{thm:noblocks}). What remains is a
support that is either smooth, with $d$ one of four values, or singular and
Cohen--Macaulay, in both cases with a Hilbert--Burch resolution over a bundle
that is not projectively flat and a restriction
$H^{2}(\cO_{X})\to H^{2}(\cO_{Z})$ that is injective
(\Cref{cor:disjointroutes}). \Cref{rem:routemap} writes the two remaining
questions in the form a construction would have to answer.

\subsection{Weil classes of CM fields}

\Cref{sec:cmfields} removes the restriction to imaginary quadratic fields. The
arguments that turn the conjecture for a Weil family into a propagation
statement use a connected period domain, a flat class on it, a group whose
rational points act with dense orbits, and a base point at which the class is
a polynomial in divisor classes, and none of them uses that the field is
quadratic. Only the base point has to be found anew, and we find it for every
CM field $F$ of degree $2m$, every $n$ and every discriminant: the abelian
variety is isogenous to a product of CM abelian $m$-folds, and the Weil
classes there are polynomials with rational coefficients in $F$-balanced
divisor classes, written out in \Cref{thm:cmbasepoint}, hence algebraic by
the Lefschetz theorem on $(1,1)$ classes. The density of the rational orbits
needs the hermitian form to be isotropic over the totally real subfield,
which the Hasse principle gives, and then the unipotent radicals of the
parabolic subgroups generate (\Cref{thm:cmpropagation}). The Weil classes of
the higher CM fields are therefore the same propagation problem as the
quadratic ones, and the semiregularity criterion in its corrected form, met
at one base point of each family, would close each of them. Nothing in that
section depends on Markman's question or on a secant construction.

The families are linked by descent and scalar extension (\Cref{prop:descent}).
A product with a member of a family with $n=1$, of any discriminant, followed
by a push-forward against its Weil class, which is a divisor class, carries
the Weil classes of one family onto those of every family of the same field
in lower dimension and of any discriminant; and a scalar extension carries the
Weil classes of a CM field of degree at least four onto those of the
imaginary quadratic fields it contains. So the propagation statement is needed
only for the split families of the CM fields of degree at least four, in
infinitely many dimensions, and the base points of nontrivial discriminant,
though they exist, are needed on no route.

The smallest case of that statement, a quartic CM field at $n=2$, is analysed
in \Cref{ssec:quartic}. Divisor classes reach the Weil classes on a
Noether--Lefschetz locus and nowhere else; the locus has codimension two when
the discriminant is trivial and is governed by a quaternion algebra over the
real quadratic subfield. For a biquadratic field Markman's theorem
reaches them on a further locus of dimension four; the Casimir class of the
natural quadratic form in degree two is algebraic if and only if the Weil
classes are; correspondences with abelian varieties whose simple factors have
dimension at most seven cannot help at a member with full Hodge group; and for
a general shape the corrected criterion asks $\dim\Ext^{2}(E,E)=112$ of one
complex, every complex of that shape having $\dim\Ext^{2}(E,E)\ge68$
(\Cref{prop:quarticnl}, \Cref{prop:quarticscalar}, \Cref{prop:quarticcasimir},
\Cref{prop:quarticnogo}, \Cref{thm:quarticp2prime}). For every quartic
CM field, no Orlov product of sheaves on a principally polarised fourfold
with real multiplication meets the criterion with equality: the integral
classes of the ring generated by the two components of the polarisation are
cut out by one congruence modulo the different, and arithmetic modulo the
different and modulo $4$ leaves no room for a minimal object
(\Cref{prop:quarticsecant}, \Cref{lem:quarticlattice},
\Cref{prop:quarticother}, \Cref{thm:quarticobstruction}). None of this is a
new case of the conjecture: at $n=2$ the algebraicity of the Weil classes
remains open for every quartic CM field and every discriminant.

\subsection{The remaining statements}

\Cref{ssec:closuremap} sets down what separates these results from the
conjecture, and \Cref{ssec:closure} decides which parts of it matter. The
implications proved here, together with those the literature supplies, form
a finite rule set, and taking consequences in that rule set is a computation.
The conjecture is not among the consequences: nothing in this paper, combined
with the theorems it quotes, implies it (\Cref{thm:closure}(i)). The route
through this paper needs three open statements (\Cref{cor:fourremain}). The
first, (F1), is the propagation statement: the semiregularity criterion of
\Cref{thm:semiregclosure} in the corrected form of \Cref{rem:p2prime},
carried to every CM field in \Cref{thm:cmpropagation} and needed, by
\Cref{prop:descent}, only for the split families of the CM fields of degree
at least four. The second, (F2), is the algebraicity of the Hodge classes on
abelian varieties that divisor classes and Weil classes do not generate. The
third, (F3), is the conjecture for the varieties that are not abelian, and it
is not a remainder: those varieties include $A\times\PP^{1}$ for every abelian
variety $A$, and the conjecture for $A\times\PP^{1}$ gives it for $A$, so (F3)
is equivalent to the whole conjecture (\Cref{prop:f3ishc}). The statement that
belongs in its place is weaker. It is the conjecture modulo abelian varieties,
(F3$'$): every Hodge class is algebraic modulo the images of Hodge classes of
abelian varieties under algebraic correspondences (\Cref{def:f3prime}). It
holds on every variety whose cohomology is reached from abelian varieties in
that way, a class that contains curves and abelian varieties and is closed
under products and surjective images; it holds on a variety of dimension $k$
in degrees $0$, $2$, $2k-2$ and $2k$, and on every variety of dimension at
most three (\Cref{prop:f3prime}). Beyond that it is open, and its standard
open case, the square of a K3 surface, turns on the same Kuga--Satake
correspondence as the Mumford classes below (\Cref{rem:f3primeopen}). It is
at least as strong as the motivatedness of every Hodge class
(\Cref{prop:f3primestrength}). On the powers of a variety whose middle
cohomology has Hodge group $\SO(V)$, $t=\dim V$, the Hodge conjecture holds up
to the power $t-1$, and the first open instance, of the conjecture and of
(F3$'$) alike, is a single class, the determinant of $V$ on the $t$-th power;
for a very general K3 surface it lies on $S^{21}$ (\Cref{prop:orthpowers}).

With (F3$'$) in the rule set there are exactly five minimal sets of open
statements that yield the conjecture (\Cref{thm:closure}). Two restate the
conjecture: (F3) alone, and the Lefschetz standard conjecture for every
variety together with the motivatedness of every Hodge class
(\Cref{prop:lefmot}). The other three consist of (F3$'$) together with the
Lefschetz standard conjecture, with the variational Hodge conjecture for
algebraic classes, which the Lefschetz standard conjecture implies
(\Cref{prop:lefvhc}, \Cref{thm:vhcab}), or, along the route of this paper,
with (F1) and (F2). Only the last uses a theorem of this paper. Every
statement in the five except (F1) is a consequence of the conjecture, and the
Lefschetz standard conjecture, which lies in two of the sets and gives the
whole abelian half, is the target at their centre (\Cref{rem:bullseye}). The
secant route, to which much of this paper is devoted, lies in no minimal set:
granted every demand it makes, it reaches the families of trivial
discriminant, because the member it is built on is a product and a product
has trivial discriminant, and through descent every imaginary quadratic
family, but no CM field of higher degree (\Cref{rem:secantplace}).%
\footnote{Markman \cite{Mar25c} extends the secant construction to CM fields
of higher degree, on $X\times\widehat{X}$ for an abelian variety $X$ with real
multiplication; the semiregularity of the resulting sheaves is not addressed
there. See (C3) in \Cref{ssec:closuremap}. The secant route of this paper,
and of its rule set, is the imaginary quadratic one.}
The computation behind these statements is item (XXXIII) of
\Cref{sec:verification}, and it is part of the formal certificate of
\Cref{sec:lean}.

None of these statements is proved here in general, but the Lefschetz
standard conjecture is proved for one class of varieties. By a mechanism going
back to Tankeev
\cite{Tan03}, for an abelian scheme over a curve whose invariant cycles are
algebraic the operator $\Lambda$ is the sum of a relative Pontryagin product
with $\ell^{g-1}/(g-1)!$ and an operator along the base built from the
invariant cycles, so it is algebraic; this covers the total space of every
Mumford family (\Cref{prop:pontryaginlambda}, \Cref{thm:lefschetzfamily},
\Cref{cor:mumfordlefschetz}). With Markman's theorem the same holds for every
abelian scheme over a curve of relative dimension at most four whose invariant
classes are Hodge, and for every fibre power of a family of abelian fourfolds
of Weil type, or of sixfolds of Weil type of trivial discriminant, with
connected monodromy $\SU(V,H)$ (\Cref{cor:lefschetzsmall}, which uses the
Hodge conjecture for all powers of such varieties, \Cref{prop:powers}). Over a
compact Shimura curve of torsion-free level the total space of a Mumford
family satisfies the Hodge conjecture (\Cref{prop:mumfordfivefold}). The same
decomposition shows that for such a total space the Lefschetz standard
conjecture is equivalent to the propagation of algebraicity along the curve
(\Cref{thm:lefschetzpropagation}). With the base points of this paper this
turns each of the two remaining targets into the Lefschetz standard
conjecture for one variety: one Weil family is equivalent to it for the total
space of the family over a curve through a base point (\Cref{cor:weilequiv}),
and the first classes beyond the Weil lines are equivalent to it for the
ninefold $W\times_{C}W$ of a Mumford family (\Cref{cor:mumfordequiv}). At
every CM point $c$ of a Mumford family the Hodge conjecture holds for
$X_{c}\times X_{c}$, as a consequence of the Hodge conjecture for abelian
fourfolds (\Cref{thm:mumfordcm}).

Nor are (F2) and (F3$'$) reductions, to Weil classes and to abelian
varieties, in any sense that would help. The self-product of an abelian
fourfold of Mumford type has eight Hodge classes in $H^{4}$ against six
products of divisor classes, and the two classes left over are Weil classes
of nothing (\Cref{prop:mumfordproduct}); and the $H^{3}$ of a very general
quintic threefold is not of abelian type, because the Hodge cocharacter acts
on the Lie algebra of its Mumford--Tate group with a weight that no tensor
construction on abelian varieties can produce (\Cref{prop:noabeliantype}).
Each of the two is the conjecture for a class of Hodge classes that no Weil
line reaches.

\Cref{sec:beyond} presses the first of them one step further. The two classes
on the square of a Mumford fourfold are the real multiplication by a totally
real cubic field on the primitive part of $H^{2}$, and they are algebraic as
soon as the Kuga--Satake class of one K3 surface of Picard number thirteen
attached to the fourfold is algebraic; the Clifford relations of the
Kuga--Satake construction bring the whole field into play
(\Cref{prop:mumfordrm}, \Cref{thm:mumfordks}, \Cref{rem:mumfordks}). That
Kuga--Satake class is not known to be algebraic. If the two classes are
algebraic, so is every Hodge class on every power of the fourfold
(\Cref{thm:mumfordpowers}), the one class that pull-backs and products do not
reach being obtained by composing correspondences. The Kuga--Satake class
asks for more than the two classes: it is algebraic if and only if they are
and some algebraic cycle links the surface to a power of the fourfold
(\Cref{prop:mumfordkslink}), and no such link passes through an abelian
variety of dimension at most five (\Cref{prop:mumfordcarrier}).

The two classes are rigid. In each of the $46$ weight blocks of $HT^{2}$ of
the square the Gram determinant of contraction into a rational exceptional
class is a product of sums of squares and of linear forms that do not vanish
at the conjugates of an irrational element of the cubic field, so the only
first order deformation keeping the class of Hodge type is the direction of
the compact Mumford curve, and no degeneration or larger family reaches it
(\Cref{thm:mumfordrigid}, \Cref{cor:nodegeneration}). Propagation along the
curve follows from one perfect complex at one CM point with $\Ext^{2}$ of
dimension $119$, the least its Chern character allows, or from a degree bound
at infinitely many CM points (\Cref{thm:mumfordnumerical},
\Cref{cor:mumfordbounded}). Such an object is heavily constrained: for a
dense set of classes its self-extensions have dimension at least
$1,16,119,328,560,328,119,16,1$ in degrees $0$ to $8$, the Euler
characteristic forces at least $800$ in odd degrees when there are none in
negative degrees, its Chern character lies outside the subring generated by
divisor classes, and it extends along no Hochschild direction but that of
the curve (\Cref{thm:mumfordprofile}, \Cref{prop:mumforddivisor},
\Cref{cor:mumfordsplit}). No sequence of cones, tensor products, pull-backs,
push-forwards and Fourier--Mukai functors applied to line bundles reaches the
classes at any point of the curve, because every such operation preserves
invariance under the Lefschetz group (\Cref{thm:lefschetzclosure}).

Among all complex tori, algebraic or not, the component of the Hodge locus
through the square is the Mumford curve itself, so no twistor line through a
member keeps the classes of Hodge type, and Markman's twistor mechanism for
Weil classes cannot move them; the twistor lines on which they are of Hodge
type carry non-algebraic tori (\Cref{prop:notwistor}). The square is a
holomorphic symplectic variety whose symplectic class generates a copy of the
structure of K3 type in $H^{1}\otimes H^{1}$, and the classes are the dual
forms of that copy twisted by the real multiplication; so the mechanism of
van Geemen and Rapagnetta \cite{vGR26}, pull-back from a hyperk\"ahler
manifold, would need a rational map onto a symplectic subvariety of a
hyperk\"ahler manifold of dimension at least ten, such as $S^{[n]}$ with
$n\ge5$ for a K3 surface of Picard number thirteen, and no hyperk\"ahler
eightfold serves (\Cref{prop:hksquare}). By Li's proof of the Tate conjecture
for powers of abelian fourfolds over finite fields \cite{Li26}, the classes
are represented by algebraic cycles at every closed point of every reduction
of the curve modulo a prime, and the target is equivalent to a bound on the
Hilbert data of those cycles on a Zariski dense set of closed points of the
arithmetic model, with no lifting step (\Cref{prop:modp},
\Cref{thm:modpdensity}). \Cref{rem:bypassaudit} examines the ten routes to
the target that avoid the Lefschetz standard conjecture, and none of them is
carried out. The five that are not closed off are not five obligations: three
are the target in other forms and two imply it, so any one would suffice
(\Cref{cor:mumfordone}). The target is only the first case of (F2), so a
proof of it would leave open (F1), the other classes of (F2), and (F3), which
is the whole conjecture (\Cref{rem:mumfordneeded}, \Cref{prop:f3ishc}).

\Cref{sec:final} collects all of this in a single theorem of seven clauses,
each of them unconditional: the reduction of the conjecture for one member to
one class, and for a family to one class on one connected domain, with its
converse; a base point with an explicit cycle in every family, for every
field and every discriminant; one criterion for propagation, which is a
number, and one that restates the conjecture; the fourteen routes that are
closed, with the structural reason each of them fails; the same criterion for
every CM field; and the three statements that remain along this route,
beside the other minimal routes.

\subsection{Computations}

Every numerical assertion in the paper is checked in exact arithmetic, over
$\QQ$, over its finite extensions or modulo a prime, and no step is numerical
in the floating-point sense; \Cref{sec:verification} lists the checks item by
item. They include the closed form of the cohomological Fourier--Mukai
transform on powers of $\theta$ and the stability of $\QQ[\theta]$ under it,
the numerical question of \Cref{cor:nonumerical} for $2\le n\le8$, the
identity $\sum_{q}h^{q,q+2}=\binom{4n}{2n-2}$ for the semiregularity target of
\Cref{sec:semireg}, the character separation of \Cref{thm:character}, the
coordinate model and the two generators \eqref{eq:omegagens}, the invariant
count behind \Cref{thm:hdgcount}, the Clifford arithmetic of \Cref{sec:ks},
and the family and the secant plane of \Cref{sec:construction}, together with
the finite computations on which the later sections rest. The finite
arithmetic on which many of the theorems turn is checked a second time,
independently, by the kernel of the Lean~4 proof assistant, and
\Cref{sec:lean} describes this formal certificate.

\subsection{Organisation}

\Cref{sec:conventions} fixes conventions. \Cref{sec:weiltype} develops the
structure theory of abelian varieties of Weil type from the definitions,
\Cref{sec:explicit} writes the Weil classes in coordinates and counts the
Hodge classes, and \Cref{sec:characters} proves \Cref{thm:character}.
\Cref{sec:fm} establishes the closed form of the cohomological
Fourier--Mukai transform, \Cref{sec:divisor} proves \Cref{thm:divisor} and
\Cref{cor:neverweil}, and \Cref{sec:numerology} shows that the numerical
count obstructs nothing. \Cref{sec:semireg} computes the semiregularity
target, proves that the objects with Chern character concentrated on the Weil
line on which $H^{2}(\cO_{A})$ acts faithfully are not semiregular, and
identifies the kernel with the tangent space to the family.
\Cref{sec:hyperplane} proves \Cref{thm:hyperplane} and \Cref{thm:returntrip},
\Cref{sec:ks} marks the boundary of the Kuga--Satake route, and
\Cref{sec:reduction} proves \Cref{thm:equivalence}. \Cref{sec:escape}
identifies the hypotheses a construction must break and how Markman's
construction breaks them, and \Cref{sec:construction} writes the secant plane
down in every dimension.

\Cref{sec:basepoints} constructs a base point in every Weil family and
reduces the conjecture for a family to propagation. \Cref{sec:propagation}
proves that the algebraic locus is a dense countable union of closed sets and
develops the two propagation criteria. \Cref{sec:corrected} proves that the
semiregularity criterion in its pure form is never met, computes the
corrected form, and examines which objects can meet it. \Cref{sec:secantobjects} treats the secant objects on the split
member, Markman's candidate in dimension eight, and the supports that such
objects can have. \Cref{sec:cmfields} extends the base points and the
propagation criteria to every CM field and analyses the quartic case.
\Cref{sec:scope} delimits each result, proves the descent lemma and the cases
of the Lefschetz standard conjecture described above, and computes the
closure of the implications; \Cref{sec:beyond} studies the first Hodge
classes beyond the Weil lines, on the square of a Mumford fourfold; and
\Cref{sec:final} states the closure theorem. \Cref{app:exterior} carries the
coordinate computation behind the Fourier--Mukai transform, \Cref{app:cdk}
the theorem of Cattani, Deligne and Kaplan on the locus of Hodge classes, and
\Cref{app:albert} the place of Weil type in Albert's classification.
\Cref{sec:verification} records the computations and \Cref{sec:lean} the
formal certificate.
